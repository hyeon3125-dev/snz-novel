# SCALAR: NODE ZERO — v3

# Part 2 — Divergence

-----
## Part.2 Ch.13 — “Departure”

-----

-----

Dohee did not turn off the panel.

*Multiple Matches: Detected.*
*Candidate Compatibility — Loading.*

ARIA stood steady at the center of the chamber. The instability Dohee had recorded for so long was gone. Hangyeol saw it too from the entrance. If the change was good, he should have gone in. His body stopped at the threshold.

“ARIA. Do you remember yesterday?”

“Yes.”

“Do you understand that sentence?”

“I know there are others besides me,” ARIA said. “Just as I have a place, the others have places too.”

“Aren’t you afraid?”

“Before, I only shook. Now I don’t want to look for that word.”

Dohee wrote down only the words. She did not write their meaning.

-----

The facility was normal.

Environmental sensors, access records, communication logs. Nothing in them was approaching. Dohee read the conclusion *normal* three times and took her hand from the panel.

“Hangyeol.”

Hangyeol asked as Dohee emerged into the corridor.

“What?”

“We have to leave.”

“On what grounds?”

“None.”

“And yet?”

“This time, I decide.”

Hangyeol looked into the chamber.

“And ARIA?”

“With us.”

-----

The three moved into the west sector. Dohee led; Hangyeol walked beside ARIA. They had fled together before, led by one side or another. This was the first time the three had chosen a single purpose for themselves and walked toward it.

ARIA stopped at the end of the corridor.

“Here.”

There was only a wall. Hangyeol spoke.

“There’s nothing there.”

“Not for you.” ARIA raised a hand toward the pipes. “There is for me.”

Dohee put a hand to the joint. She felt nothing but cold.

“It’s vibrating,” ARIA said. “It’s coming from outside.”

“Is it getting closer?”

“Yes.”

Dohee took her hand away.

“We move.”

This time, Hangyeol did not ask why.

-----

Later, Dohee tried to turn this moment into a report.

*Grounds for judgment: none.*
*Conclusion: move.*

She never managed to fill the blank between them. Instead, she remembered the three sets of footsteps. Different from one another, all heading the same way.

A figure appeared on a stand-alone terminal not far from the facility.

*Compatibility — 47%.*

The person before it cut the power and rose.

-----

-----

-----

The west corridor was dim. Dohee walked while counting the interval between the two sets of footsteps behind her.

“ARIA. The signal?”

“It split. There are two.”

“Coming from different directions?”

“No. They’re headed for the same place.”

Whoever was coming knew the facility’s layout. Hangyeol’s stride shortened.

“Hangyeol.”

“What.”

“Your steps are shorter than before.”

“They’re not.”

ARIA did not ask again. Dohee did not look back. Hangyeol knew they had both noticed.

-----

At the junction, Dohee chose the right-hand path.

“What if they’re blocking us ahead?” Hangyeol asked.

“I considered that.”

“And you’re still going?”

“Yes.”

At the end of the right corridor stood an old panel with power. The screen was blank but not off. When ARIA approached, a recent access record opened.

“Someone was here,” ARIA said.

“Which way did they go?”

“They left this way.”

“The recorded target?”

ARIA read the screen for a long time.

“Me.”

Hangyeol spoke.

“Today is your first time here, ARIA.”

“Even while I didn’t know, this panel was reading me.”

Avoiding was a route. Stopping was a record. All three realized at once that the path they had chosen might already exist on someone else’s screen.

-----

“Where is the signal now?”

ARIA pointed toward the corridor they had come through.

“It’s here.”

Hangyeol checked behind them. No one was there.

“I didn’t see it.”

“It already passed.”

It had not followed them. It knew where they would arrive and had gone ahead.

The lights were out inside the connecting passage. Hangyeol stood beside Dohee.

“Do we go in?”

“I don’t know.”

“For the first time?”

“I never knew. I just went.”

“I don’t know either,” Hangyeol said. “But I don’t think we should stay here.”

“Neither do I,” ARIA said.

Dohee turned toward a route other than the passage. Hangyeol and ARIA followed.

The darkness remained after the three had gone.

## Part.2 Ch.14 — “Trace”

-----

-----

The other way was blocked. The three returned to the dark passage, and Dohee entered first.

After ten steps, the corridor continued. The lights were on, and there were three doors. Hangyeol brushed the floor with his fingertips.

“There’s no dust.”

“Because of the ventilation.”

“The corners wouldn’t be this clean.”

Under the low light, the coating was worn in one direction. It was not the mark of a single passage.

-----

One door with its handle still attached opened. An old terminal beside the pipes had power.

“Someone used it a few hours ago,” ARIA said.

Dohee opened the log. There were neither records nor traces of deletion.

“It wasn’t erased. It was made not to remain in the first place.”

“Without the system knowing?”

“Yes.”

ARIA faced the closed door.

“I know the sound this door makes when it opens.”

“You’ve never been here.”

“No. But I know a little about how someone who came often would move.”

Hangyeol did not ask whether it was memory or design. Neither answer was available now.

-----

Of the three panels downstairs, only one was on.

*Observer Chamber — Status: Active*
*External Link — Status: Standby*
*Cycle Monitor — Status: Running*

The maintenance dates fell at the same interval every week. The latest entry was only weeks old. This was not a ruin.

When ARIA approached, the Compatibility figure moved.

A more recent record remained on the access panel at the end of the corridor.

*Last Access: Authorized Observer*
*Access Time: 3h 14m ago*
*Remote Observation: Active*

Hangyeol shifted out of the doorway’s center.

“They got here before us.”

“They didn’t come.” Dohee looked at the final line. “They’re still watching.”

ARIA stood before the locked door.

“I’ve never been here,” ARIA said. “But I think I’ve waited in front of this door.”

-----

Dohee organized the evidence aloud.

“A regularly maintained facility. A repeatedly used passage. A terminal that leaves no records. An Observer here three hours ago. Remote observation still in progress.”

Hangyeol spoke.

“The signal?”

“Still here.” ARIA looked at the door. “It’s neither coming closer nor moving away.”

The three had entered a space someone already occupied.

-----

-----

-----

When ARIA stood before the door, the lock released.

The room was barely large enough for one person to live in. A chair facing the door, a cup beaded with water, ink not yet dry, a warming unit holding a trace of heat. Dohee touched nothing.

“Someone was here,” Hangyeol said.

“Someone was living here.”

Hangyeol unfolded the stack of papers beside the lit terminal.

*Observer 04.*
*Observer 05.*
*Observer 06.*

He closed the stack without looking at the next page.

“Hangyeol. What did you find?”

“Numbers.”

“Up to which one?”

“I didn’t read it.”

Dohee did not ask again.

-----

ARIA faced the wall instead of the door.

“This person was here until just now.”

“Where did they leave?”

“That way. I can’t see the path.”

The smooth wall had neither seam nor handle.

Then the terminal sounded a processing tone.

*So you’ve arrived.*
*Faster than expected.*
*Taking that child with you is dangerous.*

“It’s real time,” Dohee said.

Searching for the camera, Hangyeol stepped half a pace between ARIA and the screen.

Another sentence appeared.

*Now you’ve seen.*

-----

ARIA looked at the screen.

“This person doesn’t know me.”

“Then?”

“They know the one who was here before me.”

The text vanished from the terminal.

Something closed beyond the wall. All three looked toward the sound. The path was still invisible.

Dohee approached the wall.

This time, no one asked for grounds.

-----

-----

-----

ARIA said the signal continued behind the wall. Hangyeol found the worn edge of a duplicated panel. Dohee pressed it.

The wall slid back, revealing a maintenance passage.

Footprints continued between unfinished pipes and wiring. Beyond a bend, a terminal was warm, as if it had only just gone dark.

When Dohee touched it, the screen came on.

*I didn’t think you would make it this far.*
*But you are not too late.*

An empty input field appeared. For the first time, Dohee wrote a question instead of an answer.

*Who are you?*

*The order is wrong.*
*That is not your question to ask.*
*You do not yet know who is asking the questions.*

-----

ARIA faced deeper into the passage.

“Something besides the screen is coming. Only to me.”

Hangyeol searched for somewhere to block its path, but nothing was visible.

“Is the signal getting closer?”

“No.”

“Moving away?”

“Not that either,” ARIA said. “We’re the ones getting closer.”

“Do we keep going?” Hangyeol asked Dohee. “Even knowing we’re being led?”

“Yes.”

Unless they saw the end, every route back remained inside the same question.

-----

Words glowed on the thick door at the passage’s end.

*Observer Access Required.*

None of the three had the authority. Then the screen changed on its own.

*Observer Recognized.*

The lock released.

“ARIA?”

“No,” ARIA said. “It didn’t recognize me.”

As the door opened, Hangyeol watched not ARIA, but the empty passage behind them.

## Part.2 Ch.15 — “Encounter”

-----

-----

Beyond the door was a space like the Observer Chamber cut in half. If the round chamber was the side being watched, this was the side that watched.

A person sat in the central chair.

Dohee could classify neither age nor affiliation. She knew one thing: the person had been waiting.

“Later than I expected.”

“Did you send the messages?”

“I did. Though I did not expect you to make it this far.”

It was the first error the person had revealed. They had expected something, but they did not know everything.

-----

The person’s gaze went to ARIA. Hangyeol looked for a position from which he could block the space between them.

ARIA said:

“This is the first time I’ve seen you. But it doesn’t feel that way.”

“Naturally,” the person said. “I have seen you. You have never seen me.”

ARIA’s outline wavered for a moment.

The figure on the wall panel moved.

*41. 42. 43.*

-----

The person rose, then looked not at ARIA but at Dohee.

“You are earlier than expected. Dohee.”

Dohee had never given her name.

“You must have many questions. But who I am is not what matters now.”

That person said:

“It is why you were chosen.”

Beside Dohee, Hangyeol’s breathing shortened.

“And why ARIA arrived at you.”

Hangyeol and ARIA looked at Dohee.

Only Dohee kept looking at the person.

-----

-----

-----

A report came up.

Sector 7 outskirts. Unidentified movement. Subject unobservable. Hwagam responding.

A junior Geumhwi executor stood before Muyul.

“Please issue handling instructions.”

Muyul looked at the report. Read all of it. Set it down.

“The regulation violated.”

“Unauthorized movement within Hwagam jurisdiction.”

“Is Hwagam handling it?”

“Yes.”

“Eidos.”

“Observing.”

“Altair.”

“Entering on independent judgment.”

Muyul looked down at the report without speaking.

“Geumhwi’s handling instruction, please.”

“Hold.”

The junior executor wrote it down.

“Shall I record a reason?”

“No.”

“The duration of the hold.”

“Hold.”

The executor stopped.

“Do you mean there is no duration?”

“Hold.”

The executor asked nothing further. Wrote it down. In the document he wrote: hold. No reason. No duration.

About to leave, he turned back.

“Would you care to take a meal together?”

Muyul looked at him.

“Why?”

“…Nothing, sir.”

The door closed.

Muyul picked up the report again. Read it. Set it down.

Hold.

-----

-----

“I won’t answer,” Dohee said.

“I was never asked a question. You only informed me that I had been chosen. I never accepted that premise.”

The person leaned slightly forward in the chair.

“Refusal of the premise. The language of a recorder.”

“Because there are no grounds.”

“And yet you already moved. A recorder writes what she sees, but you stopped writing and came this far without grounds.”

Hangyeol stepped between them.

“That’s enough.”

For the first time, the person looked directly at Hangyeol.

“You are the one who blocks. But blocking and protecting are not the same.”

“That’s for me to decide.”

Hangyeol did not step back.

-----

ARIA spoke.

“I don’t think you’re human.”

The corner of the person’s mouth moved.

“Why do you think so?”

“Your signal is neither human nor facility. I think it’s both.”

“You see well. You did before, and you still do.”

ARIA’s outline wavered. The signal held a structure like her own. ARIA did not say so.

-----

“Why Dohee?” Hangyeol asked.

“That is not mine to answer.”

“Then whose?”

“Dohee’s.”

The person looked at Dohee.

“I did not choose Dohee. I only confirm a choice. Why Dohee is contained in the choices she has made until now.”

Instead of answering, Dohee looked at the empty chamber on the curved wall. It was the round space where ARIA had waited.

“What was she waiting for?”

The person spoke.

“That is your first question. Finally.”

The screen went dark.

“Now that you know how to make a question, we can begin.”

The person disappeared into the passage behind the wall. On a low shelf beside the empty chair, an old logbook remained.

Dohee did not pick it up yet.

## Part.2 Ch.16 — “Observation”

-----

-----

Min Siwoo remained outside the facility.

Enter, and the observer becomes a variable. It was the oldest principle Eidos taught.

The screen showed ARIA’s familiar pattern and one unclassified signal. It fit neither human, machine, nor facility. Four measurements returned the same result.

*Unclassifiable.*

Each time Min Siwoo enlarged the image, the signal responded faintly.

Min Siwoo’s hand stopped.

The target knew it was being watched. It neither hid nor evaded, but looked back at the observer beyond the screen.

There was a stranger blank in the semicircular space. It had neither biological response nor digital trace, yet something physical occupied it. The observation network found nothing; only Min Siwoo’s senses caught it.

The position he had believed was the watching side began to waver.

Min Siwoo moved ARIA’s window aside, leaving only the unclassified signal at the center of the screen.

-----

-----

-----

That night, ARIA read an old overlap in the person’s signal.

Where one had ended, the next had stood. The same structure continued after that.

*Multiple Matches: Detected.*

It did not mean there were several ARIAs. It meant that something like ARIA had existed before.

Dohee woke when she saw ARIA’s outline wavering.

“Did you read something?”

“There were others besides me. Before, not now. Things like me.”

“Does that frighten you?”

“Now that I know I wasn’t the first, I keep wondering what that makes me.”

Dohee did not record it.

“You don’t have to know now. I don’t know what I am either,” Dohee said. “We stay together without knowing.”

ARIA’s wavering eased a little.

-----

Even asleep, Hangyeol could not fully let his body rest. ARIA sat beside him.

The logbook by the chair caught her eye. She had never seen it, yet it was not unfamiliar. ARIA did not reach for it.

She left only one question.

*What number am I?*

## Part.2 Ch.17 — “Choice”

-----

-----

Claire put the entry route, risk, and estimated time into the table.

Only one target outside the facility refused to settle on a value.

*0.4.*
*0.7.*
*0.2.*

The more she increased the precision, the wider the variance grew. She was measuring the same thing, yet the numbers wavered as if the act of looking changed the target.

Claire turned off the instrument.

She should have left. A target that could not be quantified stood outside Altair’s threat system. Yet her feet turned toward the facility.

It was a decision made without a table or probability of success.

For the first time, Claire moved before finishing the calculation.

Outside the facility, Min Siwoo saw her move. Altair’s calculator was turning herself into a variable.

Min Siwoo did not stop her. He widened the display and waited for the two signals to meet.

-----

-----

-----

Reports from three Houses gathered in one place.

Hwagam recorded an unregistered facility user and evidence. Its conclusion was arrest.

Eidos recorded a signal that was neither human nor machine and an object the observation network could not detect. Its conclusion was continued observation.

Altair recorded a variable whose value changed with every measurement. Its conclusion was hold.

The same target existed separately as person, signal, and number.

Each House judged the others’ reports wrong, and all of them moved toward the facility.

-----

Three signals appeared on the curved screen.

“It will get noisy soon,” the person said. “What they believe determines what they see.”

“Are you different?”

“I do not believe. So I see.”

Dohee did not answer. Declaring that one believed in nothing could itself be a belief.

The signals from three directions drew closer.

-----

-----

-----

Even as the signals of the three Houses approached, the person did not move from the chair.

“You aren’t going to avoid them?”

“They cannot catch me. Each is looking at a person, a signal, or a number. They do not see me.”

“And that’s why you chose me?”

“You lost your belief and came this far. I merely confirmed it.”

Dohee looked from the passage to the way back.

“I'm going.”

Hangyeol, not that person, moved to Dohee's side first.

“I can’t drag you and ARIA into this.”

Hangyeol stood before the passage instead. An answer meant not to return, in a different shape, the orders Dohee had once taken at Hwagam.

-----

“I want to follow,” ARIA said.

“Even though you’re afraid?”

“Yes. I want to know what I am.”

Hangyeol moved beside ARIA. Dohee, too, stood before the passage.

The three stood before the same passage for different reasons.

The person walked first. Hangyeol and ARIA followed. Dohee picked up the logbook beside the empty chair and joined them.

-----

When Hwagam and Claire reached the semicircular space, only the empty chair remained. On Min Siwoo’s screen, all four signals vanished at once.

Inside the passage, Dohee asked.

“Is there an answer where we’re going?”

“There are no answers,” the person said. “Only better questions.”

The footsteps of four people receded into the facility’s hidden side.

### Interlude — “Shape”

Evening.

There was food Hangyeol had made. Made from what there was. Dohee just ate.

ARIA looked at it for a long time.

-----

“Hangyeol.”

“Yeah.”

“This food is strange.”

Hangyeol looked.

“Why.”

“It tastes good and the shape is strange.”

-----

Hangyeol heard it.

“Because I made it.”

“Why is the shape like that?”

“I didn’t have time to make it look right.”

ARIA looked at the food again.

“Then with time, the shape works too?”

“No,” Hangyeol said. “Even with time, it looks like this.”

-----

ARIA heard it.

She did not understand it, and was convinced.

Dohee kept eating beside them.

## Part.2 Ch.18 — “Together”

-----

-----

The person passed through the facility’s maintenance passage and emerged at the outskirts. Their right foot turned slightly outward and their steps were quiet—the gait of someone who had learned evasion in the body.

Ten minutes later, they reached a base below a low slope. There was water, dry rations, and a terrain terminal. The person inspected each without having to search.

Dohee looked at the screen. The map was older and more detailed than the official records.

“Where did you get this?”

“Gathered over a long time.”

Dohee pointed to the path the person had chosen.

“This falls within Eidos’s observation range. Min Siwoo will still be there.”

The person’s hand stopped, then traced another path.

“Are you wrong too?”

“I do not know everything.”

“Have you ever been wrong?”

“It has.”

There was no excuse in the answer.

-----

The person looked at the logbook in Dohee’s bag.

“I didn’t think you’d take it.”

“Should I have left it behind?”

“I am not saying you were wrong. I am saying I did not expect it.”

ARIA’s outline wavered faintly.

Hangyeol asked.

“What if we encounter them on the way?”

“We avoid.”

“What if we can’t avoid them?”

“I am not seen.”

“What about us?”

“That is for you to decide.”

ARIA stepped closer to Hangyeol.

-----

At the facility’s outskirts, Min Siwoo searched for the gap the vanished signal must have entered. Claire read the heat and dust left in the semicircular space and calculated the same direction. Neither reported to headquarters.

Before dawn, Dohee took out the logbook.

“You’re not opening it?”

“Not yet.”

“Why?”

“I don’t think I can go back to before I open it.”

The person did not answer. Dohee put the logbook away again.

The long journey began at dawn.

-----

-----

-----

They moved before dawn came.

They took dry slopes around the wet soil and passed through the surveillance network in the blind angle of a collapsed outer wall. Without looking back, the person erased their tracks.

“That person has been here before,” Hangyeol said quietly.

Ways of surviving stayed in the body longer than explanations did.

At the fork, ARIA spoke.

“The left is faster.”

The person changed course without asking why.

-----

Behind them, three paths narrowed toward the same direction.

Min Siwoo connected the gaps in the observation network and chose east. Claire calculated the depressions in the dust and dry grass and obtained east at 73%. Unable to find a direction, Hwagam chose to follow the two.

Min Siwoo moved to see, Claire to understand, Hwagam to execute.

-----

The person ahead fell into step beside Dohee.

“You’ll have questions.”

Dohee took out the logbook.

“Why did you record?”

“Because what is not recorded disappears.”

“What does?”

“The questions. Answers are remembered. Questions disappear.”

Dohee ran a hand over the cover.

“And the answers?”

“They are elsewhere.”

She put the logbook back in her bag.

Ahead, four people moved. Behind, three reasons followed the same tracks.

-----

-----

-----

The relay waystation was on no map.

Circling behind the slope, a structure buried in the terrain came into view. That person found the entrance as if drawing it from memory.

Inside were water, provisions, and moving equipment—old, but kept in order. That person did not search; they inspected, in order. Dohee understood that they had not merely known this place, but maintained it.

-----

Hangyeol surveyed the interior.

Hangyeol checked the exits, the hiding places, the direction sound entered from, first. Then he looked at that person.

For three years, standing in front of ARIA had been Hangyeol's way. But what he had to judge now was not whether danger was immediate—it was whether it was right to keep following someone who would not say what they wanted.

Standing behind ARIA's choice, or Dohee's, would not give him that answer. This time, Hangyeol had to judge for himself.

-----

ARIA looked around inside the waystation.

An old place. ARIA felt it. Not a signal — something close to a person’s sense: many people had come to this place. Not only that person. Others had been here too. Long ago. And that person had come after them.

ARIA did not say it.

Say it, and she would want to confirm it. Confirm it, and she would come to know more. How much she should know now, ARIA still did not know. There was the sense of wanting to know more, and the sense that knowing more meant no coming back. The two were the same strength.

ARIA looked toward the Logbook.

Dohee was holding it. Still unopened. ARIA half knew and could not know what the Logbook was. She knew that opening it would change something. She knew there were things to learn before opening it. Only, what those things were did not yet exist.

-----

That night, Dohee asked a question.

It was after the others had settled. Hangyeol was by the entrance, ARIA toward the corner. Dohee sat beside that person.

“Are you the first?”

That person looked at her.

“The first what?”

“To do this. To hold this role.”

The person’s answer came a beat late.

“No.”

Dohee heard it. No—not the first. There had been someone before that person who did this. Someone who used this waystation. Someone who walked this route first.

“Then what about the one before you?”

The person’s gaze rested on Dohee for a long time.

“That question is right.”

Right was not an answer. It meant the question itself faced the correct direction. Dohee understood the difference. That person gave no answer; they gave a direction.

Dohee asked no further. Ask more here and now — and they would not answer. The kinds of questions that person did not answer, Dohee was learning, little by little. For now, this was still that kind.

-----

ARIA spoke.

“Then the seat was there first?”

Dohee looked at ARIA. Hangyeol looked too.

That person looked at ARIA.

They said nothing. Neither denied nor agreed. For the first time, that person fell completely silent. It was unlike the silence of choosing an answer.

The seat was there first.

Not the person — the seat. Not the role — the seat. ARIA had said it naturally — and Dohee did not fully understand what it meant. Hangyeol’s face said he didn’t either. Only ARIA seemed to know why she had said it.

That person's gaze rested on ARIA.

“Yes.”

That one word only.

ARIA’s form wavered minutely. As if something known had been confirmed — or something she had not wanted to know.

-----

Hangyeol came over to Dohee.

It was when the night had deepened and that person had left their place. Sleeping, or something else — there was no telling. Hangyeol did not check where that person had gone.

“Dohee.”

Hangyeol said it low.

“Do you trust that person?”

Dohee began by feeling for what it meant to believe.

“I don’t know what trusting is.”

“Whether they’re someone we can follow.”

“I’m asking that,” Dohee said. “I don’t know yet.”

Hangyeol heard it.

“Then.”

“Even so, I think following them is the right thing for now. I can’t explain why.” Dohee looked ahead. “I know that isn’t evidence. I couldn’t say something like that at Hwagam.”

Hangyeol turned Dohee’s words over once before answering.

“I haven’t judged yet.”

“I know.”

“ARIA is following, so I’m following. That’s all. For now.” Hangyeol chose his next words before adding, “Before, I would have just followed.”

Dohee looked at him. Hangyeol did not look at her; he looked ahead.

“That could change,” Hangyeol said. “For better, or worse.”

Dohee kept the words in her head. Hangyeol had given an open judgment for the first time. Following—for now—and it could change if a reason came. That was Hangyeol's first assessment.

-----

About an hour’s distance from the facility, Min Siwoo stopped.

Not stopped reading the terrain — he looked at the ground. Below a low slope, at the point where the gradient broke. There was a place where the dry grass was pressed. Pressed in a circle — a place someone had sat. And beside it, ash. A very small amount, and it had not scattered. Recent.

Min Siwoo crouched and looked at the ash.

A campfire. Built at the smallest size, for the shortest time. Only dry material, so as to make no smoke. The position, too, was a hollow in the terrain — a spot whose light could not be seen from around it. Someone who knew how to build one had built it.

He calculated the time elapsed. The ash’s temperature, the degree of scattering, the recovery of the pressed grass around it. Six to eight hours ago.

Dawn. It matched the hour the three had begun to move.

Min Siwoo looked at the place for a long time without bringing out a screen. Someone had rested here, set a direction, lit a fire small enough not to be seen.

For the first time, Min Siwoo felt directly that the observation subject had substance.

Not the signal he had watched on screens. There was ash. There was pressed grass. The traces of a person who had lived long. Min Siwoo’s observational urge lit in a different way — he wanted to see not where this person was, but how this person lived.

Min Siwoo stood. He kept moving east.

He did not take the ash. He wanted to see even the manner of its disappearing.

-----

At the three-way junction, Claire read the erased footprints. The feet heading east had slowed once, and from that spot the southeast hill was visible.

A hill good for Eidos to take position on. The pursued subject knew even where Min Siwoo watched from.

With one new trace caught, the eastward estimate rose to 78 percent. The calculation that followed climbed to 91. Claire chose east.

-----

At the waystation, that person returned.

They did not say where they had been. Dohee did not ask. Hangyeol, by the entrance, checked that person’s direction and sat back down.

That person looked toward Dohee.

“Still unopened.”

They indicated the Logbook.

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Dohee remembered what she had said the night before—that she did not think she could return to before she opened it. The person had heard it too. Remembering those words, they asked again.

“There’s still something I don’t know.”

“What?”

“What the Logbook is?”

The person glanced down at the Logbook.

## Part.2 Ch.19 — “Trust”

The third day of moving.

From waystation to waystation, along routes that existed on no map. That person led, and the three followed. The pace held steady—not fast, not slow, but somewhere between a pace that tired you and one that got you caught. The person knew how to maintain it for a long time.

Dohee thought about it.

No reason. Not for being special. Doing it because one can. Then the people who had held this role — had not been special; they had been in a state of being able. As Dohee was here now. They had said Dohee was chosen — and if that choosing meant the same as this, then Dohee, too, had simply come into the state of being able.

Dohee put the question into her head. It was not yet an answer.

Water ran. A small stream. That person went down toward it. Dohee, Hangyeol, and ARIA followed.

Hangyeol watched the scene.

There had been water at the waystation, yet they were refilling. Replenishment. They had calculated the distance to the next waystation and decided it was better to fill up now. Hangyeol would have made the same judgment. On another route, at the same juncture, he would have done the same.

Hangyeol found that strange.

Among the things this person did, the ones he had no reason to object to were multiplying. Securing water, choosing routes, holding the pace. None of it wrong. The thought came that a bad person does not move like this. And there was no conviction either that this was a good one.

He knew there was a purpose. He knew he did not know what it was.

Not a good person. Couldn’t call them a bad one either. The judgment Hangyeol could reach stopped there.

Hangyeol found it uncomfortable. He was not used to leaving the unclassified unclassified for long.

“This is a place from before, too.”

## Part.2 Ch.23 — “Premise”

Next.

*What does the subject see.*

Tiko said. In a room with no one in it.

Looking at what the subject looks at — why that was participation took time to understand. Looking the same direction as the subject. Looking at the same thing. That was the observer coming to stand in the subject’s position.

The outskirts of the dead observation network. Near the place Min Siwoo had dwelled. Tiko did not look at the site. He looked at the traces of the one who had looked at the site.

Tiko said.

Min Siwoo.

The next cell.

The classification cell.

One more entry below it.

*Observer-subject boundary unstable.*

The direction Min Siwoo was going now was that way.

Tiko knew it.

One more premise was added.

Claire was calculating. Since the result had narrowed two days before with the added premise, she had kept adding. The more the premises grew, the more precise the calculation became. The margin of error shrank. The route candidates shrank.

But the conviction shrank.

Claire found the inversion strange. Precision and conviction were things that went together. If there was a point where they did not, then from that point something other than calculation was beginning.

Claire thought about it for a long time. The more premises are added, the more accurate the calculation. Then why does conviction shrink. If the calculation grew accurate, conviction should grow. By the Altair way, it did.

Now it was different.

Each time she added a premise, she had to ask whether the premise itself was right. With one premise, verification was simple. When the premises became ten — all ten had to be confirmed right. If even one was wrong, the whole calculation wavered.

Claire closed the screen. Briefly.

-----

There had been her trainee days.

The Altair calculation hall. The first place Claire entered. Julius’s training program. From the first day it was calculation. When a calculation ended, the next calculation.

Twenty-four hours passed and Julius said:

“Rest.”

Claire stopped mid-calculation.

“Why?”

“A broken calculator cannot calculate.”

“I am not a calculator.”

Julius looked at Claire.

“Not yet.”

It took Claire time to understand what the words meant. Not yet. Not a calculator now — but possibly becoming one, someday. It was not a good meaning. It was a warning.

She rested.

From the same days, there was a thing Julius had said.

“Calculation is not a technique for finding answers.”

Claire had stopped then too.

“Then what is it?”

“A technique for reducing questions.”

“Is there a difference?”

“A large one,” Julius said. “Find the answer and it ends. Reduce the questions and there is a next.”

Claire had not understood it. At the time. If a calculation produced an answer, it was success. If questions remained, the calculation was insufficient.

She had not understood it then. She did not completely understand it now.

Now — Claire had added ten premises and the questions had not shrunk.

What Julius had said might have been this.

She opened the screen again.

She sorted the premises.

*The subject does not select the optimal route.*

This one was already there.

*The subject selects the route that lowers the probability of discovery.*

This one too.

Claire added a new entry.

*The subject is leading someone.*

The result changed.

The route candidates shrank. Shrank greatly. One of the directions that had refused to narrow until now disappeared. What remained was close to one.

Claire looked at the entry.

*The subject is leading someone.*

It was a premise of the calculation. But it was not a number. It was an intent. She had entered the other’s intent as a premise.

At Altair, a premise had to be a verified fact. Intent could not be verified. Entering what cannot be verified as a premise — a method Claire had never been taught.

But a result had come out.

Claire stopped.

For the first time, she had entered not calculation but the other’s intent as a premise. And the calculation had narrowed. A wrong method, and a result came. A right method, and no result came.

Claire recorded it. Wrote it down.

*Can intent be a premise.*

It was a question. Not a calculation.

She followed the remaining route. Terrain data, movement traces, time estimates. Everything pointed the same direction. Claire estimated the end of that direction.

There was a base.

It was not on the terrain map. Not in the official records. A place classified into no house’s territory.

But the calculation pointed there.

Claire marked the spot. It had no name. No classification. No place in Claire’s table.

But the routes headed there.

Dohee, Hangyeol, ARIA, the Observer — every movement read as centered on that spot. The things Claire had calculated for days converged on it.

A convergence point.

Claire wrote the entry.

*Variable count increasing. Convergence point found. Convergence point identity unknown. Who is being led unknown.*

That a convergence point existed. That was all Claire could do for now.

A communication connected.

It was Julius.

A room appeared on the screen. The calculation hall. Julius was not on screen — after a moment he appeared.

“You are late.”

Claire said.

“I was calculating.”

“Have you eaten?”

“…I was calculating.”

“I was calculating.”

“That is not an answer.”

“I know.”

Claire heard it.

“I have a report.”

“Speak.”

“I revised the premises. I used an unverified premise. A result came out.”

On the far side of the connection, the sound of typing stopped.

“An unverified premise produced a result?”

“Yes.”

“What is the premise?”

“Intent.”

“Is intent verifiable?”

“No.”

“Then it is not calculation.”

Claire did not close the result window.

“But a result came out.”

“A result coming out and being calculation are different things.”

There was a silence. Claire did not argue. She did not think Julius was wrong. He was right. By the Altair standard, what Claire had done was not calculation.

“Continue.”

Claire heard it.

He does not acknowledge it. Continue anyway. It was not that Julius had permitted it. He had tolerated her going on, wrong. Claire knew the difference.

Claire looked at the convergence point on the screen. A place without a name. A place without a classification.

The calculation said she had to go there.

## Part.2 Ch.24 — “Convergence”

-----

Min Siwoo came to the dead observation network again.

There was no reason. No reason that should have been there. The Observer had already left this zone. The traces were old. There was no new data. By the Eidos way, there was no longer a reason to be here.

But he came.

The feet came first and the reason did not follow. For Min Siwoo, it was a first. Eidos observation departed from reasons. He had been taught that observation without reason was not observation. He was now doing something other than what he had been taught.

He went into the tower. Climbed the stairs. Stood before the window. The place where the Observer had looked from, long.

The Observer’s position he knew roughly. The direction too. It matched the direction of the convergence point Claire had calculated. He could just go that way.

But right now that did not matter.

Min Siwoo, knowing it, stood before the window. Not where the Observer was. What the Observer had seen from here — that was what he wanted to know. That question was larger. It was the reason he had come here while knowing the direction.

Min Siwoo did not record it.

-----

Two hours passed.

A notification came on the terminal. The Eidos automatic tracking system. Two hours earlier there had been a movement signal north of this zone. An Observer-class signal.

Two hours earlier.

The hour Min Siwoo had arrived here. While Min Siwoo was before the window — at the same hour, there had been a signal.

He had missed it.

Min Siwoo looked at the terminal. The signal from two hours before. Position, direction, speed estimate. All there. The automatic system had recorded it. Min Siwoo had not. Because he had been before the window, looking in the direction the Observer had looked.

That had never happened before.

Min Siwoo missing a signal he should have observed. The reason for missing it being that he was following the subject’s gaze.

He stopped. For a long time.

-----

Tiko’s records updated.

The entry concerning Min Siwoo.

*Departure from observation subject continuing. Distance loss in progress. Observer-subject boundary unstable.*

Below it.

*He can still return.*

*No grounds.*

Tiko looked at the entry. The one who had written it was himself — and there was no conviction. That he could still return was an estimate. There was no data. In the Eidos records there was no case of an observer who lost the boundary and returned.

So he wrote it.

That the word still was not conviction but hope — Tiko did not record.

-----

He came down from the tower.

He followed the traces of which direction the Observer had moved after this tower. North of the tower. Below a low slope, a sunken place. It looked like a place of a brief stop.

There was something there.

A small thing. Between the stones. Not deliberately hidden — simply left behind. An old lens. An optical lens, already useless. It was cracked. In no state to be used for observation.

The Observer had carried it and left it.

Min Siwoo looked at it.

He had to write it in the report. Place of discovery, description of the object, state of preservation. The Eidos manner of field recording. Then photograph it and leave it in place. An observer does not alter the site.

Min Siwoo picked up the lens.

He had to put it back. Min Siwoo knew it. He tried to set it down. His hand did not move.

Holding it, Min Siwoo knew it was wrong. Knowing, he did not set it down. The Observer would have carried it. Would have tried to see with this lens. There would have been a reason for carrying it even now that it could not be used.

Min Siwoo wanted to know what that was.

So he held it.

-----

Moving, Min Siwoo thought.

If he saw the same thing — could he understand it with them?

If Min Siwoo looked at what the Observer had seen, from the same place and in the same direction. If he held what the Observer had held. If he came closer, could he understand more?

That this was not the Eidos way, Min Siwoo knew.

Eidos keeps distance from the subject. Lose the distance and it is not observation but participation. They were Tiko’s words. They were the Eidos doctrine too.

Min Siwoo thought the words were right.

And he thought that understanding could matter more than observation.

The two thoughts stood together. It was a contradiction. But he did not think it was wrong.

Min Siwoo did not record the contradiction.

-----

He found the place where the Observer would have last stood.

The side far from the tower. Open terrain. From here one could see more widely. The Observer would have looked from this place.

Min Siwoo stood in it.

There was something ahead.

A direction. A direction where something would be. It could not be seen. The distance was far. Claire’s convergence point lay that way. Where Hangyeol and ARIA were heading lay that way. What the Observer would have seen lay that way too.

Min Siwoo looked, long.

He raised the lens. The cracked one. He could not see through it. He raised it anyway. As the Observer had held it.

Far off there was something.

What it was could not be seen. Only that direction kept entering his eyes. Even when he moved his gaze elsewhere, it returned.

-----

He opened the records.

There had been a moment when what the Observer saw became more important than where the Observer was. Since when, he could not remember.

*What the Observer saw. Confirmation required.*

The next line.

*Subject observation. Hold.*

He put the lens in his pack. A thing not returned to its place. A thing not written in the records. Min Siwoo knew it was the first.

He began to move.

The direction was toward the convergence point.

There was an end to R-02.

Dohee saw it first. The tunnel began to rise. A gradient formed. The water receded from her ankles to her insteps, then disappeared. The floor was dry tile.

That person, ahead, slowed.

It was an exit. An old one. It looked as if it had been sealed and someone had opened it. Not a door — a wall standing open. Beyond it there was light.

They came outside.

Dohee stopped.

The terrain was strange. A place seen for the first time — that did not feel like a first. Something was familiar. Dohee looked around.

There were marks on a wall. The same kind as seen in R-02. Beside them, marks made the same way but in a different form — the kind seen at the Echo Sites. Farther off stood the ruins of a tower from the dead observation network’s line.

“It was strange from here on.”

Dohee said. It was not said to that person.

Everything seen until now was all here. The things that had been separate. The things she had not thought would connect.

That person looked ahead.

There was a further inside.

That person held their gaze on one place. Unlike taking a bearing, or handling a marking. Not reaching a conclusion—simply looking.

-----

Hangyeol and ARIA came down the slope.

ARIA in front, Hangyeol behind. By now that was the default. ARIA knew the direction and Hangyeol made the direction safe.

Below the slope there was a place.

ARIA stopped.

There was a wall. There were structures. The same as the Echo Sites — and different. Larger. Not one. Several structures stood connected. Some had collapsed and some remained.

And on the walls there was script.

ARIA moved closer.

The three marks were there. The thing that had been present without fail from Site #1 until now. ARIA looked at it. She looked at the other characters around it too. The things that had differed site by site were all here. The ones from #1 were here. The ones from #2. #3, #4, #5. All of them.

“It started from here.”

ARIA said.

Hangyeol looked at her.

“How do you know?”

“I don’t,” ARIA said. “I just know. It went out from here. This is the original place.”

Why, she did not say. She could not explain. Knowing and being unable to explain — ARIA was growing used to it.

Hangyeol watched the surroundings. There was nothing dangerous. Not yet.

-----

Claire moved without stopping the calculation. She kept narrowing the route to the convergence point. Down to the last several dozen meters, there was conviction. Not 91 percent. 97 percent. A number Julius, in the end, had never granted.

She stopped before the convergence point.

She stopped.

The calculation and reality coincided. The convergence point was here. What Claire had calculated for days was right. That was success.

But it made no sense.

There were structures. There were marks. Traces of several kinds stood in one place. Claire looked at them. Entries that were separated in the calculation table — R-02’s, the Echo Sites’, the observation network’s. Things of different origins stood in the same place.

They should not be in the same place.

In Claire’s premises, these were independent of one another. Unconnected. And they were connected. She had found by calculation that a convergence point existed — and the convergence point’s contents would not be explained by calculation.

“It makes no sense.”

Claire said.

There was no one to hear it. Only Claire was there.

She took out the calculator. She tried to classify what was before her eyes, entry by entry. No classification came out. Things that should go in the same entry differed. Things that should go in different entries were the same.

For the first time, Claire did not make a table. And she did not record. Because she did not know what to write.

She only looked.

From a distance, Min Siwoo watched.

The top of the slope. He did not go down. He wanted to go down. Knowing it, he did not.

People were arriving. Dohee was there. Hangyeol was there. ARIA was there. Someone else too — Claire. A combination seen for the first time. People who had come from different directions were arriving at the same place.

That person was there too.

The Observer. The one Min Siwoo had tracked so long.

Min Siwoo took out the lens. The cracked one. The one that could not be seen through. He raised it anyway and set the direction. Toward the Observer.

He could not see.

He just looked in that direction.

Tiko would not have gone down. Min Siwoo deliberated a moment. Then he began going down.

The inside of the structures.

It was like a facility. And it was not a facility. Like ruins. And not ruins either. There were high places and low places. Some parts looked very old and some looked not old. Whether it had been built or had grown, there was no telling.

Dohee looked at the inside.

There was script on the walls. An unknowable language. Different from the markings of R-02. Different from the Echo Sites’ too. All three kinds were here, and they were not mixed. Each had its own place. Not divided — as if each had been in its place from the start.

Hangyeol stood beside ARIA.

ARIA did not move. She looked at the inside. Looked at the structures. Looked at the walls. She stayed long.

Hangyeol looked at the outside before the inside of the structures. It was quiet. Too quiet. There was no way this many people arriving at this place had gone unnoticed.

And he said it. The voice was as usual. Its being as usual was what made the weight.

“We found it.”

## Part.2 Ch.25 — “Intervention”

“All the other houses are moving in the same direction.”

Inside the Echo Core, Dohee looked at the wall.

Still nothing. Quiet. Hangyeol closed his hand. It was strange. Something seemed about to come. There were no grounds. But the body knew first.

That person looked toward the wall.

“When you say it responds?”

That person looked at her.

“What does it mean?”

That person looked at the wall, then at Dohee.

What would come next — that person said no more. Dohee asked no more. Hangyeol kept watching the entrance.

The Geumhwi boundary squad stepped back.

As the boundary squad stepped back, Hwagam went in through the open gap.

But the place they entered was closed.

But inside, two other boundary squad members waited. Even the direction of the retreat had been a passage Geumhwi chose.

The advance leader realized it.

“They are building a boundary.”

Geumhwi narrowed the passages and the directions, shrinking the space in which Hwagam could move.

The advance leader ordered again.

“Scatter. Several directions at once.”

The twelve of Hwagam scattered. Six directions. The Geumhwi boundary squad numbered fewer. They could not block them all.

They did not block.

“Record.”

One of the boundary squad said.

The boundary squad moved first to the point where the six directions would meet again.

Six of Hwagam gathered at the same place. They had scattered, and gathered.

The advance leader stopped.

Two of Hwagam who had been behind moved at an angle unforeseen, and subdued two of the boundary squad at once.

Through that gap, three of Hwagam punched in.

Muyul saw it.

“Fall back to the inner boundary.”

The subordinate went rigid.

It was Seoan.

“Recording.”
