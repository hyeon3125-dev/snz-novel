# SCALAR: NODE ZERO — v3

# Part 2 — Divergence

-----
## Part.2 Ch.13 — “Departure”

-----

-----

Dohee did not turn off the panel.

*Multiple Matches: Detected.*
*Candidate Compatibility — Loading.*

ARIA stood steady at the center of the chamber. The instability Dohee had recorded for so long was gone. Hangyeol saw it too from the entrance. If the change was good, he should have gone in. His body stopped at the threshold.

“ARIA. Do you remember yesterday?”

“Yes.”

“Do you understand that sentence?”

“I know there are others besides me,” ARIA said. “Just as I have a place, the others have places too.”

“Aren’t you afraid?”

“Before, I only shook. Now I don’t want to look for that word.”

Dohee wrote down only the words. She did not write their meaning.

-----

The facility was normal.

Environmental sensors, access records, communication logs. Nothing in them was approaching. Dohee read the conclusion *normal* three times and took her hand from the panel.

“Hangyeol.”

Hangyeol asked as Dohee emerged into the corridor.

“What?”

“We have to leave.”

“On what grounds?”

“None.”

“And yet?”

“This time, I decide.”

Hangyeol looked into the chamber.

“And ARIA?”

“With us.”

-----

The three moved into the west sector. Dohee led; Hangyeol walked beside ARIA. They had often shared a space, but this was the first time they had walked in the same direction.

ARIA stopped at the end of the corridor.

“Here.”

There was only a wall. Hangyeol spoke.

“There’s nothing there.”

“Not for you.” ARIA raised a hand toward the pipes. “There is for me.”

Dohee put a hand to the joint. She felt nothing but cold.

“It’s vibrating,” ARIA said. “It’s coming from outside.”

“Is it getting closer?”

“Yes.”

Dohee took her hand away.

“We move.”

This time, Hangyeol did not ask why.

-----

Later, Dohee tried to turn this moment into a report.

*Grounds for judgment: none.*
*Conclusion: move.*

She never managed to fill the blank between them. Instead, she remembered the three sets of footsteps. Different from one another, all heading the same way.

A figure appeared on a stand-alone terminal not far from the facility.

*Compatibility — 47%.*

The person before it cut the power and rose.

-----

-----

-----

The west corridor was dim. Dohee walked while counting the interval between the two sets of footsteps behind her.

“ARIA. The signal?”

“It split. There are two.”

“Coming from different directions?”

“No. They’re headed for the same place.”

Whoever was coming knew the facility’s layout. Hangyeol’s stride shortened.

“Hangyeol.”

“What.”

“Your steps are shorter than before.”

“They’re not.”

ARIA did not ask again. Dohee did not look back. Hangyeol knew they had both noticed.

-----

At the junction, Dohee chose the right-hand path.

“What if they’re blocking us ahead?” Hangyeol asked.

“I considered that.”

“And you’re still going?”

“Yes.”

At the end of the right corridor stood an old panel with power. The screen was blank but not off. When ARIA approached, a recent access record opened.

“Someone was here,” ARIA said.

“Which way did they go?”

“They left this way.”

“The recorded target?”

ARIA read the screen for a long time.

“Me.”

Hangyeol spoke.

“Today is your first time here, ARIA.”

“Even while I didn’t know, this panel was reading me.”

Avoiding was a route. Stopping was a record. All three realized at once that the path they had chosen might already exist on someone else’s screen.

-----

“Where is the signal now?”

ARIA pointed toward the corridor they had come through.

“It’s here.”

Hangyeol checked behind them. No one was there.

“I didn’t see it.”

“It already passed.”

It had not followed them. It knew where they would arrive and had gone ahead.

The lights were out inside the connecting passage. Hangyeol stood beside Dohee.

“Do we go in?”

“I don’t know.”

“For the first time?”

“I never knew. I just went.”

“I don’t know either,” Hangyeol said. “But I don’t think we should stay here.”

“Neither do I,” ARIA said.

Dohee turned toward a route other than the passage. Hangyeol and ARIA followed.

The darkness remained after the three had gone.

## Part.2 Ch.14 — “Trace”

-----

-----

The other way was blocked. The three returned to the dark passage, and Dohee entered first.

After ten steps, the corridor continued. The lights were on, and there were three doors. Hangyeol brushed the floor with his fingertips.

“There’s no dust.”

“Because of the ventilation.”

“The corners wouldn’t be this clean.”

Under the low light, the coating was worn in one direction. It was not the mark of a single passage.

-----

One door with its handle still attached opened. An old terminal beside the pipes had power.

“Someone used it a few hours ago,” ARIA said.

Dohee opened the log. There were neither records nor traces of deletion.

“It wasn’t erased. It was made not to remain in the first place.”

“Without the system knowing?”

“Yes.”

ARIA faced the closed door.

“I know the sound this door makes when it opens.”

“You’ve never been here.”

“No. But I know a little about how someone who came often would move.”

Hangyeol did not ask whether it was memory or design. Neither answer was available now.

-----

Of the three panels downstairs, only one was on.

*Observer Chamber — Status: Active*
*External Link — Status: Standby*
*Cycle Monitor — Status: Running*

The maintenance dates fell at the same interval every week. The latest entry was only weeks old. This was not a ruin.

When ARIA approached, the Compatibility figure moved.

A more recent record remained on the access panel at the end of the corridor.

*Last Access: Authorized Observer*
*Access Time: 3h 14m ago*
*Remote Observation: Active*

Hangyeol shifted out of the doorway’s center.

“They got here before us.”

“They didn’t come.” Dohee looked at the final line. “They’re still watching.”

ARIA stood before the locked door.

“I’ve never been here,” ARIA said. “But I think I’ve waited in front of this door.”

-----

Dohee organized the evidence aloud.

“A regularly maintained facility. A repeatedly used passage. A terminal that leaves no records. An Observer here three hours ago. Remote observation still in progress.”

Hangyeol spoke.

“The signal?”

“Still here.” ARIA looked at the door. “It’s neither coming closer nor moving away.”

The three had entered a space someone already occupied.

-----

-----

-----

When ARIA stood before the door, the lock released.

The room was barely large enough for one person to live in. A chair facing the door, a cup beaded with water, ink not yet dry, a warming unit holding a trace of heat. Dohee touched nothing.

“Someone was here,” Hangyeol said.

“Someone was living here.”

Hangyeol unfolded the stack of papers beside the lit terminal.

*Observer 04.*
*Observer 05.*
*Observer 06.*

He closed the stack without looking at the next page.

“Hangyeol. What did you find?”

“Numbers.”

“Up to which one?”

“I didn’t read it.”

Dohee did not ask again.

-----

ARIA faced the wall instead of the door.

“This person was here until just now.”

“Where did they leave?”

“That way. I can’t see the path.”

The smooth wall had neither seam nor handle.

Then the terminal sounded a processing tone.

*So you’ve arrived.*
*Faster than expected.*
*Taking that child with you is dangerous.*

“It’s real time,” Dohee said.

Searching for the camera, Hangyeol stepped half a pace between ARIA and the screen.

Another sentence appeared.

*Now you’ve seen.*

-----

ARIA looked at the screen.

“This person doesn’t know me.”

“Then?”

“They know the one who was here before me.”

The text vanished from the terminal.

Something closed beyond the wall. All three looked toward the sound. The path was still invisible.

Dohee approached the wall.

This time, no one asked for grounds.

-----

-----

-----

ARIA said the signal continued behind the wall. Hangyeol found the worn edge of a duplicated panel. Dohee pressed it.

The wall slid back, revealing a maintenance passage.

Footprints continued between unfinished pipes and wiring. Beyond a bend, a terminal was warm, as if it had only just gone dark.

When Dohee touched it, the screen came on.

*I didn’t think you would make it this far.*
*But you are not too late.*

An empty input field appeared. For the first time, Dohee wrote a question instead of an answer.

*Who are you?*

*The order is wrong.*
*That is not your question to ask.*
*You do not yet know who is asking the questions.*

-----

ARIA faced deeper into the passage.

“Something besides the screen is coming. Only to me.”

Hangyeol searched for somewhere to block its path, but nothing was visible.

“Is the signal getting closer?”

“No.”

“Moving away?”

“Not that either,” ARIA said. “We’re the ones getting closer.”

“Do we keep going?” Hangyeol asked Dohee. “Even knowing we’re being led?”

“Yes.”

Unless they saw the end, every route back remained inside the same question.

-----

Words glowed on the thick door at the passage’s end.

*Observer Access Required.*

None of the three had the authority. Then the screen changed on its own.

*Observer Recognized.*

The lock released.

“ARIA?”

“No,” ARIA said. “It didn’t recognize me.”

As the door opened, Hangyeol watched not ARIA, but the empty passage behind them.

## Part.2 Ch.15 — “Encounter”

-----

-----

Beyond the door was a space like the Observer Chamber cut in half. If the round chamber was the side being watched, this was the side that watched.

A person sat in the central chair.

Dohee could classify neither age nor affiliation. She knew one thing: the person had been waiting.

“Later than I expected.”

“Did you send the messages?”

“I did. Though I did not expect you to make it this far.”

It was the first error the person had revealed. They had expected something, but they did not know everything.

-----

The person’s gaze went to ARIA. Hangyeol looked for a position from which he could block the space between them.

ARIA said:

“This is the first time I’ve seen you. But it doesn’t feel that way.”

“Naturally,” the person said. “I have seen you. You have never seen me.”

ARIA’s outline wavered for a moment.

The figure on the wall panel moved.

*41. 42. 43.*

-----

The person rose, then looked not at ARIA but at Dohee.

“You are earlier than expected. Dohee.”

Dohee had never given her name.

“You must have many questions. But who I am is not what matters now.”

That person said:

“It is why you were chosen.”

Beside Dohee, Hangyeol’s breathing shortened.

“And why ARIA arrived at you.”

Hangyeol and ARIA looked at Dohee.

Only Dohee kept looking at the person.

-----

-----

-----

A report came up.

Sector 7 outskirts. Unidentified movement. Subject unobservable. Hwagam responding.

A junior Geumhwi executor stood before Muyul.

“Please issue handling instructions.”

Muyul looked at the report. Read all of it. Set it down.

“The regulation violated.”

“Unauthorized movement within Hwagam jurisdiction.”

“Is Hwagam handling it?”

“Yes.”

“Eidos.”

“Observing.”

“Altair.”

“Entering on independent judgment.”

Muyul looked down at the report without speaking.

“Geumhwi’s handling instruction, please.”

“Hold.”

The junior executor wrote it down.

“Shall I record a reason?”

“No.”

“The duration of the hold.”

“Hold.”

The executor stopped.

“Do you mean there is no duration?”

“Hold.”

The executor asked nothing further. Wrote it down. In the document he wrote: hold. No reason. No duration.

About to leave, he turned back.

“Would you care to take a meal together?”

Muyul looked at him.

“Why?”

“…Nothing, sir.”

The door closed.

Muyul picked up the report again. Read it. Set it down.

Hold.

-----

-----

“I won’t answer,” Dohee said.

“I was never asked a question. You only informed me that I had been chosen. I never accepted that premise.”

The person leaned slightly forward in the chair.

“Refusal of the premise. The language of a recorder.”

“Because there are no grounds.”

“And yet you already moved. A recorder writes what she sees, but you stopped writing and came this far without grounds.”

Hangyeol stepped between them.

“That’s enough.”

For the first time, the person looked directly at Hangyeol.

“You are the one who blocks. But blocking and protecting are not the same.”

“That’s for me to decide.”

Hangyeol did not step back.

-----

ARIA spoke.

“I don’t think you’re human.”

The corner of the person’s mouth moved.

“Why do you think so?”

“Your signal is neither human nor facility. I think it’s both.”

“You see well. You did before, and you still do.”

ARIA’s outline wavered. The signal held a structure like her own. ARIA did not say so.

-----

“Why Dohee?” Hangyeol asked.

“That is not mine to answer.”

“Then whose?”

“Dohee’s.”

The person looked at Dohee.

“I did not choose Dohee. I only confirm a choice. Why Dohee is contained in the choices she has made until now.”

Instead of answering, Dohee looked at the empty chamber on the curved wall. It was the round space where ARIA had waited.

“What was she waiting for?”

The person spoke.

“That is your first question. Finally.”

The screen went dark.

“Now that you know how to make a question, we can begin.”

The person disappeared into the passage behind the wall. On a low shelf beside the empty chair, an old logbook remained.

Dohee did not pick it up yet.

## Part.2 Ch.16 — “Observation”

-----

-----

Min Siwoo remained outside the facility.

Enter, and the observer becomes a variable. It was the oldest principle Eidos taught.

The screen showed ARIA’s familiar pattern and one unclassified signal. It fit neither human, machine, nor facility. Four measurements returned the same result.

*Unclassifiable.*

Each time Min Siwoo enlarged the image, the signal responded faintly.

Min Siwoo’s hand stopped.

The target knew it was being watched. It neither hid nor evaded, but looked back at the observer beyond the screen.

There was a stranger blank in the semicircular space. It had neither biological response nor digital trace, yet something physical occupied it. The observation network found nothing; only Min Siwoo’s senses caught it.

The position he had believed was the watching side began to waver.

Min Siwoo moved ARIA’s window aside, leaving only the unclassified signal at the center of the screen.

-----

-----

-----

That night, ARIA read an old overlap in the person’s signal.

Where one had ended, the next had stood. The same structure continued after that.

*Multiple Matches: Detected.*

It did not mean there were several ARIAs. It meant that something like ARIA had existed before.

Dohee woke when she saw ARIA’s outline wavering.

“Did you read something?”

“There were others besides me. Before, not now. Things like me.”

“Does that frighten you?”

“Now that I know I wasn’t the first, I keep wondering what that makes me.”

Dohee did not record it.

“You don’t have to know now. I don’t know what I am either,” Dohee said. “We stay together without knowing.”

ARIA’s wavering eased a little.

-----

Even asleep, Hangyeol could not fully let his body rest. ARIA sat beside him.

The logbook by the chair caught her eye. She had never seen it, yet it was not unfamiliar. ARIA did not reach for it.

She left only one question.

*What number am I?*

## Part.2 Ch.17 — “Choice”

-----

-----

Claire put the entry route, risk, and estimated time into the table.

Only one target outside the facility refused to settle on a value.

*0.4.*
*0.7.*
*0.2.*

The more she increased the precision, the wider the variance grew. She was measuring the same thing, yet the numbers wavered as if the act of looking changed the target.

Claire turned off the instrument.

She should have left. A target that could not be quantified stood outside Altair’s threat system. Yet her feet turned toward the facility.

It was a decision made without a table or probability of success.

For the first time, Claire moved before finishing the calculation.

Outside the facility, Min Siwoo saw her move. Altair’s calculator was turning herself into a variable.

Min Siwoo did not stop her. He widened the display and waited for the two signals to meet.

-----

-----

-----

Reports from three Houses gathered in one place.

Hwagam recorded an unregistered facility user and evidence. Its conclusion was arrest.

Eidos recorded a signal that was neither human nor machine and an object the observation network could not detect. Its conclusion was continued observation.

Altair recorded a variable whose value changed with every measurement. Its conclusion was hold.

The same target existed separately as person, signal, and number.

Each House judged the others’ reports wrong, and all of them moved toward the facility.

-----

Three signals appeared on the curved screen.

“It will get noisy soon,” the person said. “What they believe determines what they see.”

“Are you different?”

“I do not believe. So I see.”

Dohee did not answer. Declaring that one believed in nothing could itself be a belief.

The signals from three directions drew closer.

-----

-----

-----

Even as the signals of the three Houses approached, the person did not move from the chair.

“You aren’t going to avoid them?”

“They cannot catch me. Each is looking at a person, a signal, or a number. They do not see me.”

“And that’s why you chose me?”

“You lost your belief and came this far. I merely confirmed it.”

Dohee looked from the passage to the way back.

“I decide.”

Hangyeol answered before the person could.

“What Dohee decides is Dohee’s to decide.”

“I can’t drag you and ARIA into this.”

“Whether I follow is mine to decide. ARIA decides for herself,” Hangyeol said. “If Dohee decides for us too, that’s the same thing Hwagam did to you.”

-----

“I want to follow,” ARIA said.

“Even though you’re afraid?”

“Yes. I want to know what I am.”

Hangyeol moved beside ARIA.

“Then I’m going too. That’s my decision.”

Dohee spoke last for herself.

“I’m going too.”

The three stood before the same passage for different reasons.

The person walked first. Hangyeol and ARIA followed. Dohee picked up the logbook beside the empty chair and joined them.

-----

When Hwagam and Claire reached the semicircular space, only the empty chair remained. On Min Siwoo’s screen, all four signals vanished at once.

Inside the passage, Dohee asked.

“Is there an answer where we’re going?”

“There are no answers,” the person said. “Only better questions.”

The footsteps of four people receded into the facility’s hidden side.

### Interlude — “Shape”

-----

Evening.

There was food Hangyeol had made. Made from what there was. Dohee just ate.

ARIA looked at it for a long time.

-----

“Hangyeol.”

“Yeah.”

“This food is strange.”

Hangyeol looked.

“Why.”

“It tastes good and the shape is strange.”

-----

Hangyeol heard it.

“Because I made it.”

“Why is the shape like that?”

“I didn’t have time to make it look right.”

ARIA looked at the food again.

“Then with time, the shape works too?”

“No,” Hangyeol said. “Even with time, it looks like this.”

-----

ARIA heard it.

She did not understand it, and was convinced.

Dohee kept eating beside them.

## Part.2 Ch.18 — “Together”

-----

-----

The person passed through the facility’s maintenance passage and emerged at the outskirts. Their right foot turned slightly outward and their steps were quiet—the gait of someone who had learned evasion in the body.

Ten minutes later, they reached a base below a low slope. There was water, dry rations, and a terrain terminal. The person inspected each without having to search.

Dohee looked at the screen. The map was older and more detailed than the official records.

“Where did you get this?”

“Gathered over a long time.”

Dohee pointed to the path the person had chosen.

“This falls within Eidos’s observation range. Min Siwoo will still be there.”

The person’s hand stopped, then traced another path.

“Are you wrong too?”

“I do not know everything.”

“Have you ever been wrong?”

“It has.”

There was no excuse in the answer.

-----

The person looked at the logbook in Dohee’s bag.

“I didn’t think you’d take it.”

“Should I have left it behind?”

“I am not saying you were wrong. I am saying I did not expect it.”

ARIA’s outline wavered faintly.

Hangyeol asked.

“What if we encounter them on the way?”

“We avoid.”

“What if we can’t avoid them?”

“I am not seen.”

“What about us?”

“That is for you to decide.”

ARIA stepped closer to Hangyeol.

-----

At the facility’s outskirts, Min Siwoo searched for the gap the vanished signal must have entered. Claire read the heat and dust left in the semicircular space and calculated the same direction. Neither reported to headquarters.

Before dawn, Dohee took out the logbook.

“You’re not opening it?”

“Not yet.”

“Why?”

“I don’t think I can go back to before I open it.”

The person did not answer. Dohee put the logbook away again.

The long journey began at dawn.

-----

-----

-----

They moved before dawn came.

They took dry slopes around the wet soil and passed through the surveillance network in the blind angle of a collapsed outer wall. Without looking back, the person erased their tracks.

“That person has been here before,” Hangyeol said quietly.

Ways of surviving stayed in the body longer than explanations did.

At the fork, ARIA spoke.

“The left is faster.”

The person changed course without asking why.

-----

Behind them, three paths narrowed toward the same direction.

Min Siwoo connected the gaps in the observation network and chose east. Claire calculated the depressions in the dust and dry grass and obtained east at 73%. Unable to find a direction, Hwagam chose to follow the two.

Min Siwoo moved to see, Claire to understand, Hwagam to execute.

-----

The person ahead fell into step beside Dohee.

“You’ll have questions.”

Dohee took out the logbook.

“Why did you record?”

“Because what is not recorded disappears.”

“What does?”

“The questions. Answers are remembered. Questions disappear.”

Dohee ran a hand over the cover.

“And the answers?”

“They are elsewhere.”

She put the logbook back in her bag.

Ahead, four people moved. Behind, three reasons followed the same tracks.

-----

-----

-----

The relay waystation was on no map.

Without that person’s guiding, it could not have been found. On the low side of the terrain, just below the point where the slope broke, there was a structure. From above it looked like part of the terrain; from the side it was buried in the slope and could not be seen. There was a way in, too — not from the front, but circling around to the rear. That person walked the detour as if drawing it from memory.

Inside, there were traces.

A place used often. Water was stored, dried provisions, the basic equipment needed for moving. That was all Dohee saw, and she could tell the equipment was old. Old, and kept in order. Someone had maintained this place for a long time.

Dohee watched that person check the things.

It was the manner of confirming that what should be there was there. Not searching—inspecting. Knowing about this waystation and maintaining it were different things, and that person did the latter.

-----

Hangyeol surveyed the interior.

Hangyeol was a person who read danger first. Where the exits were, where the hiding places were, where sound entered from. He looked at those first. Then at people.

He looked at that person.

For three years, Hangyeol had protected ARIA. When there was something ARIA did not know, Hangyeol stood in front. When danger came, Hangyeol took it first. That was Hangyeol’s way of protecting ARIA, and he had believed the way was right.

But now, watching that person, Hangyeol was thinking in a different mode.

Dangerous or not — that wasn’t it. What Hangyeol was looking at was something else. What kind of person this was. Not good or bad. Not that either. They seemed neither a good person nor a bad one. That person did not fit well into any classification.

A person with a purpose. What the purpose was, Hangyeol did not yet know. They did not seem to mean the three harm — and doing no harm and being a help were also different. That person was leading the three, but whether the direction of the leading was for the three or for that person’s own sake, Hangyeol could not tell.

It seemed a judgment he would have to make.

Until now, Hangyeol had not judged. If ARIA followed, Hangyeol followed. If Dohee decided, Hangyeol took the rear. But now — whether following this person was right, it seemed Hangyeol himself would have to judge it. Following without judging was the mode Hangyeol had once guarded against. Reacting without thinking. He had meant not to, and until now, that was what he had done.

-----

ARIA looked around inside the waystation.

An old place. ARIA felt it. Not a signal — something close to a person’s sense: many people had come to this place. Not only that person. Others had been here too. Long ago. And that person had come after them.

ARIA did not say it.

Say it, and she would want to confirm it. Confirm it, and she would come to know more. How much she should know now, ARIA still did not know. There was the sense of wanting to know more, and the sense that knowing more meant no coming back. The two were the same strength.

ARIA looked toward the Logbook.

Dohee was holding it. Still unopened. ARIA half knew and could not know what the Logbook was. She knew that opening it would change something. She knew there were things to learn before opening it. Only, what those things were did not yet exist.

-----

That night, Dohee asked a question.

It was after the others had settled. Hangyeol was by the entrance, ARIA toward the corner. Dohee sat beside that person.

“Are you the first?”

That person looked at her.

“The first what?”

“To do this. To hold this role.”

The person’s answer came a beat late.

“No.”

Dohee heard it. No—not the first. There had been someone before that person who did this. Someone who used this waystation. Someone who walked this route first.

“Then what about the one before you?”

The person’s gaze rested on Dohee for a long time.

“That question is right.”

Right was not an answer. It meant the question itself faced the correct direction. Dohee understood the difference. That person gave no answer; they gave a direction.

Dohee asked no further. Ask more here and now — and they would not answer. The kinds of questions that person did not answer, Dohee was learning, little by little. For now, this was still that kind.

-----

ARIA spoke.

“Then the seat was there first?”

Dohee looked at ARIA. Hangyeol looked too.

That person looked at ARIA.

They said nothing. Neither denied nor agreed. For the first time, that person fell completely silent. Not the silence of choosing an answer — a silence as if what ARIA had said had touched something inside them.

The seat was there first.

Not the person — the seat. Not the role — the seat. ARIA had said it naturally — and Dohee did not fully understand what it meant. Hangyeol’s face said he didn’t either. Only ARIA seemed to know why she had said it.

That person looked at ARIA for a long time.

“Yes.”

That one word only.

ARIA’s form wavered minutely. As if something known had been confirmed — or something she had not wanted to know.

-----

Hangyeol came over to Dohee.

It was when the night had deepened and that person had left their place. Sleeping, or something else — there was no telling. Hangyeol did not check where that person had gone.

“Dohee.”

Hangyeol said it low.

“Do you trust that person?”

Dohee began by feeling for what it meant to believe.

“I don’t know what trusting is.”

“Whether they’re someone we can follow.”

“I’m asking that,” Dohee said. “I don’t know yet.”

Hangyeol heard it.

“Then.”

“Even so, I think following them is the right thing for now. I can’t explain why.” Dohee looked ahead. “I know that isn’t evidence. I couldn’t say something like that at Hwagam.”

Hangyeol turned Dohee’s words over once before answering.

“I haven’t judged yet.”

“I know.”

“ARIA is following, so I’m following. That’s all. For now.” Hangyeol chose his next words before adding, “Before, I would have just followed.”

Dohee looked at him. Hangyeol did not look at her; he looked ahead.

“That could change,” Hangyeol said. “For better, or worse.”

Dohee recorded it. Hangyeol had given an open judgment for the first time. Following — for now — and it could change if a reason came. That was Hangyeol’s first assessment.

-----

About an hour’s distance from the facility, Min Siwoo stopped.

Not stopped reading the terrain — he looked at the ground. Below a low slope, at the point where the gradient broke. There was a place where the dry grass was pressed. Pressed in a circle — a place someone had sat. And beside it, ash. A very small amount, and it had not scattered. Recent.

Min Siwoo crouched and looked at the ash.

A campfire. Built at the smallest size, for the shortest time. Only dry material, so as to make no smoke. The position, too, was a hollow in the terrain — a spot whose light could not be seen from around it. Someone who knew how to build one had built it.

He calculated the time elapsed. The ash’s temperature, the degree of scattering, the recovery of the pressed grass around it. Six to eight hours ago.

Dawn. It matched the hour the three had begun to move.

Min Siwoo looked at the place. For a long time. He did not bring out a screen, did not enter data. He just looked. In this place, that person had rested. In this place, had set a direction. In this place, had been cold enough, or in need enough, to light a fire.

For the first time, Min Siwoo felt directly that the observation subject had substance.

Not the signal he had watched on screens. There was ash. There was pressed grass. The traces of a person who had lived long. Min Siwoo’s observational urge lit in a different way — he wanted to see not where this person was, but how this person lived.

Min Siwoo stood. He kept moving east.

He did not take the ash. He wanted to see even the manner of its disappearing.

Claire stood at a junction.

A three-way fork. By Claire’s calculation, east and southeast were the two candidates. One of the two. The 73 percent had risen to 78 as terrain data accumulated. But 22 percent still sat on the southeast.

Claire looked at the junction floor.

The dust had been swept. East. The footprints had been erased on the move, leaving a direction of travel. The erasing had not been done at a uniform speed. At one point, the person’s stride had slowed as they erased the prints. There, they had changed direction once more.

Claire calculated the reason for the stop at that place.

The surrounding terrain. The direction sound entered from. The angle at which the sightline opened. From that place, the southeast could be checked — there would have been something to the southeast. They had checked it, and held east.

Claire looked southeast.

A hill. Good terrain for Eidos observation. Min Siwoo would have been there. This person knew Min Siwoo. Knew where Eidos watched from.

Claire’s calculation jumped from 78 percent to 91.

East. Claire was not certain. But 91 percent was close to certainty. For Claire, that was enough.

She fixed her heading east.

91 percent was not certainty. But until now, she had almost never been wrong.

At the waystation, that person returned.

“Did you open it knowing — or not?”

That person looked at her.

“A good question.”

The person looked at Dohee once more before adding:

As Dohee was not opening the Logbook. As Hangyeol was deferring his judgment. Not yet.

That person, Dohee, Hangyeol, ARIA — each thinking something different, inside the same night.

## Part.2 Ch.19 — “Trust”

The third day of moving.

From waystation to waystation, along routes that existed on no map. That person led, and the three followed. The pace held steady—not fast, not slow, but somewhere between a pace that tired you and one that got you caught. The person knew how to maintain it for a long time.

Dohee observed while walking.

There was no recording terminal. Everything accumulated only in her head: the way the person walked, chose direction, timed each stop. As Dohee gathered these things, she shaped each into a question. Not filling in answers, but making questions—the person’s earlier words kept following her. A recorder leaves answers. A questioner leaves questions.

Dohee was the questioner now.

-----

At a brief midday stop, Dohee asked.

“Why you?”

That person looked at her.

“What about me?”

“This role. You said no — that you’re not the first. Then many people have held this role, and — why is it you holding it now?”

The person spoke as though drawing out an answer long kept.

“It did not have to be me.”

Dohee looked at them.

“But you’re the one doing it.”

“The one who can, does,” that person said. “When there is someone who can, that someone ends up doing it. It is not being chosen.”

“What’s the difference?”

“The chosen one thinks there is a reason. That they were chosen for being special. But if the one who can is the one who does — there is no reason. Simply, the one who can do it does it.”

Dohee thought about it.

No reason. Not for being special. Doing it because one can. Then the people who had held this role — had not been special; they had been in a state of being able. As Dohee was here now. They had said Dohee was chosen — and if that choosing meant the same as this, then Dohee, too, had simply come into the state of being able.

Dohee put the question into her head. It was not yet an answer.

-----

That afternoon, that person stopped at a low ravine beside the route.

Water ran. A small stream. That person went down toward it. Dohee, Hangyeol, and ARIA followed.

They checked the water. Drinkable. That person took out a container — small and flat. Began filling it.

Hangyeol watched the scene.

There had been water at the waystation, yet they were refilling. Replenishment. They had calculated the distance to the next waystation and decided it was better to fill up now. Hangyeol would have made the same judgment. On another route, at the same juncture, he would have done the same.

Hangyeol found that strange.

Among the things this person did, the ones he had no reason to object to were multiplying. Securing water, choosing routes, holding the pace. None of it wrong. The thought came that a bad person does not move like this. And there was no conviction either that this was a good one.

He knew there was a purpose. He knew he did not know what it was.

Not a good person. Couldn’t call them a bad one either. The judgment Hangyeol could reach stopped there.

Hangyeol found it uncomfortable. He was not used to leaving the unclassified unclassified for long.

-----

ARIA, listening to the ravine water, said:

“This is a place from before, too.”

Dohee looked at her. That person looked too.

“How do you know?”

“I don’t,” ARIA said. “But this sound isn’t unfamiliar. The way the water bends like this. I’ve heard it before. But I think I heard it somewhere I’ve never been.”

That person closed the water container.

They said nothing. About what ARIA had said. Only, the way they looked at ARIA changed for a moment — Dohee caught it. What the look was, she could not exactly read; it seemed to be confirming. Confirming something.

ARIA, perhaps feeling the look, said no more.

The place that person had used as a waystation two days earlier.

Min Siwoo reached it in the afternoon. There were traces inside. The water container had shifted slightly from its place, one of the dried provisions had decreased. There were pressed places on the floor — four people. Different sizes. A place where four had rested together.

Min Siwoo sat in the smallest of the pressed places. ARIA. ARIA had rested in this spot.

It was interesting. There was no regret. Being two days late was not a disappointment. If anything, things were becoming more visible — how these people moved, how they hid, how they rested.

The direction of the next waystation narrowed to two possibilities. Min Siwoo marked both on his screen and left the waystation.

-----

About two hours after Min Siwoo left, Claire reached the same slope.

Before climbing, Claire looked at it.

There were traces of someone having climbed from this direction. Min Siwoo — over the past days Claire had seen the traces of someone moving the same heading while keeping a fixed gap. The traces of the Eidos manner of observing. Watching without going near. Those traces were here too.

Claire climbed.

She entered the structure. There were two sets of traces. The ones who came first — two days ago. The one who came after — this afternoon.

The second set was Min Siwoo.

Claire knew it by the footstep pattern. Enter, take in the whole, check specific points in order, sit and try the places. The way an observer processes a site. Different from Altair’s way — Altair measures, records, and leaves. An observer looks.

Someone had looked at this place first.

Claire recorded it. There was an observer moving the same heading, holding the same speed differential. Eidos. Staying ahead, continuously.

Claire revised the calculation.

The fact that this Observer had already surveyed the waystation meant their information was more accurate. It was one more confirmation that Claire’s heading was right. At the same time, a competing variable had appeared.

Claire raised her pace slightly.

If this observer arrived first, Claire could be late even with her calculation right. Claire entered the possibility into her table. It was the first case in which what she had thought sufficient might not be.

When they reached the new waystation that evening, Dohee sorted her pack.

As she took things out, the Logbook came into view.

ARIA looked at it. Hangyeol looked. That person looked. Dohee did not take the Logbook out. She sorted the other things in the pack, confirmed the Logbook in its place, and closed the pack.

It was brief. No one said anything.

-----

At night, that person erased the traces along the route.

The ones left in the day’s moving. The pressed grass, the shifted stones, the drainage marks. That person handled them in order. Not fast, and thorough.

Hangyeol watched the scene.

That Hwagam personnel were in pursuit, Hangyeol knew. Eidos and Altair, too, would be coming this way. That person erasing traces was natural.

But what Hangyeol saw was something else.

Not erasing traces — making it as if there had been nothing. Not simply hiding — as if no one had passed this route at all. That difference read to Hangyeol. This person had done this a long time. It was the manner of someone who had lived a long time as if they had never been.

Whether it was the manner of a good person or a bad one, Hangyeol still could not tell.

But Hangyeol was watching it without missing it. Before, he would not have watched. It was not that his guard had loosened, or that he had grown indifferent. The opposite — he was watching more closely.

-----

That night Dohee stayed awake a long time.

That person’s words came back. *It did not have to be me.* The one who can ends up doing it. There is no reason.

Dohee thought it through again in the recorder’s way. Having no reason and not knowing the reason were different. That person had said there was no reason — whether that was no reason existing, or not knowing the reason, or not telling it. Dohee could not separate the three.

And the same question applied to Dohee herself.

Why Dohee. When that person had said, at the encounter, that she was chosen, Dohee had refused the premise. And now Dohee was following. Refusing while following — how that was possible, Dohee had not yet sorted out.

*Why you.* The question Dohee had put to that person today. They had said it did not have to be them. Then why me. The question Dohee owed herself came out of that. From why you, to why me.

One more question had formed. She recorded it. In her head.

ARIA said quietly:

“Dohee.”

“Yeah.”

“At the ravine earlier.” ARIA searched for the source of the memory before continuing. “I said I’d heard it before.”

“I know.”

“I don’t know where from. But—I don’t think this is my first time here either. At this waystation.”

Dohee heard it.

The ravine and this waystation. Neither felt new to ARIA, though she had never been to either. It was the same kind of thing ARIA had described at the facility: inside her were things she did not remember.

The person had said they maintained this waystation. Before them, someone else had held the role. Someone else had used this place.

The reason ARIA said it was not a first — could connect to that.

Dohee knew where the thought went. She did not stop it. Only, she decided not to say it now. The state was still one of not knowing enough.

“I’ll ask later.”

“Ask who?”

Dohee looked toward that person.

“Them.”

That person looked at Dohee, then looked ahead again. Said nothing.

On the fifth day of moving, a problem arose.

The route that person had checked two days before was blocked. Not blocked — someone had passed first. Hwagam. At the route’s mouth there was a Hwagam marker. A small one, and that person saw it.

They stopped.

Dohee watched that person’s back. The manner of stopping was different. Until now, when that person stopped — it was to set a direction. This time was different. It was the manner of stopping before something not predicted.

“You were the one who said they’d be using this route.”

Hangyeol said. Quietly. It was not anger — it was confirmation.

“Yes.”

“You didn’t know Hwagam would be here?”

“I judged the probability low.”

“You knew that low and none are different, didn’t you?”

That person looked at Hangyeol. Hangyeol did not lower his eyes.

“I knew.”

“Then.”

“The prediction was wrong.”

It was short. There was no excuse. Hearing it, Hangyeol looked at that person. They had said wrong. Had judged the probability low, and the probability had realized. That person, too, is wrong sometimes.

Hangyeol knew it, and still something had not loosened.

They changed the route.

That person laid out three alternatives. Going around farther, taking rougher terrain, stopping movement by day. Dohee looked at the three. The risks of each were different.

“The first.”

Dohee said.

“The longer way around?”

“If we’re avoiding the route Hwagam expects, going around is right. Because I know Hwagam’s methods.”

That person looked at Dohee. A moment.

“Right.”

They took the first route. The longer way. It would cost another day. The provisions calculation changed — there had been margin, and conserving from now would cover it. That person did not say it. Dohee calculated it on her own.

That Hangyeol knew it without hearing the calculation — Dohee could tell.

Hangyeol came up from behind to Dohee’s side.

“Dohee, you’re on that person’s side.”

Dohee looked at him.

“Not a side. I said it because it seemed right.”

“Seeming right means that person’s word is the standard.”

Dohee did not deny that she was uncomfortable.

“Right now it’s the most information we have. If that’s uncomfortable — it’s uncomfortable for me too.”

Hangyeol looked at Dohee. Dohee tried to read what was inside the look — it was not anger. It was something else.

“I can’t trust that person.”

Hangyeol said. It was the first time he had said it directly.

“I know.”

“It’s not just talk. Really. Not yet.”

“That’s for you to decide, Hangyeol,” Dohee said. “I can’t decide it.”

ARIA, walking while listening to the exchange, stopped.

There was no reason for stopping. No signal had come. No sound had been heard. Just — a sense that said this way. A direction that was not the direction of travel. Below a low slope, the direction where a narrow ravine ran.

ARIA looked that way.

She could not explain it. Until now, when ARIA proposed a direction, there had been a signal — the signal is this way, the signal is that way. Now there was no signal. She just knew. It felt like she had to go. Why, she did not know.

ARIA moved.

Toward the direction that was not the direction of travel. Dohee was walking ahead, Hangyeol beside her, that person farther in front. ARIA did not head their way; she went down the slope.

Toward the ravine there was an old structure.

Older than the facility. The structure still stood, and there was no roof. Where a roof had once been, there was now sky. ARIA went inside.

There was something on the wall.

Scratch marks. Made by scraping with a hand — letters, in a language that could not be known. Old. Very old. But not erased. The wall was hard, and the scratched marks had remained.

ARIA looked at it.

It was not unfamiliar. A script seen for the first time — that did not feel like a first. This script, or this kind of thing, she seemed to have seen somewhere. Among the things inside ARIA, there was something that overlapped with this.

ARIA went closer.

She put her hand to the wall. Nothing changed. No signal came. It was just a wall. Yet ARIA did not take her hand away. She could not explain it. It felt as though she had to be here, if only for a moment.

From outside came Dohee’s voice.

“ARIA?”

ARIA took her hand away. She came out of the structure. Dohee was coming down the slope. Hangyeol was behind her. That person was looking down from the top of the slope.

“What was there?”

Dohee asked.

“An old place,” ARIA said. “There’s something on the wall. It looks like letters, but I don’t know what language.”

That person came down the slope. Went into the structure and looked at the wall.

They said nothing.

Dohee watched that person. They were looking at the scratched marks on the wall. Looking long. Dohee knew the time was long — it was the time it took that person to process something.

“Did you know?”

Dohee asked.

“That this place was here.”

“No.”

Dohee heard it. Again, they had not known. That person had known this route, and had not known this structure. There were things where the route was known and the inside was not.

That person left the structure.

ARIA looked at the wall one last time. Dohee saw it.

“I don’t think it ended here.”

ARIA said quietly. Whether to Dohee, or to the wall, there was no telling.

“You mean there’s more?”

“Yes. In this direction.”

That evening, before the next waystation, Hangyeol stopped.

The person was walking toward the waystation when they noticed Hangyeol had stopped and turned back.

“This way.”

“I know.”

Hangyeol said.

“I’m not going this direction.”

That person looked at him.

“Until now, you set the routes,” Hangyeol said. “And today you were wrong. You said you didn’t know Hwagam would be there.”

“Yes.”

Having said it, Hangyeol knew for the first time that a judgment of his own had formed. Not that he could not trust that person — that he trusted ARIA. That was the judgment Hangyeol could make.

“So I’m going to check this way and come back.”

Hangyeol pointed the direction they had come from. Toward the route that had been blocked.

“I’ll confirm where Hwagam is, my way. Just give me one rendezvous point.”

The person looked in the direction Hangyeol indicated.

“You may not rejoin.”

“I know.”

“Yes.”

Hangyeol said. He looked at ARIA.

“ARIA.”

ARIA looked at him.

“Want to come with me?”

ARIA went to Hangyeol’s side.

Dohee saw it. ARIA had gone to Hangyeol’s side. That person and Dohee on one side, Hangyeol and ARIA on the other.

That person gave Hangyeol the coordinates of the rendezvous point. Two days later. That person said no more. Dohee said nothing either. Hangyeol began moving first. ARIA followed.

The person walked ahead toward the waystation.

Dohee watched Hangyeol and ARIA recede. ARIA looked back toward Dohee once. Dohee received the look. ARIA turned and followed Hangyeol.

Hangyeol chose to split up. Dohee chose the Observer. For the first time, it occurred to Dohee that her choice might be wrong. At Hwagam, she had guarded against writing false records—this was a different kind of wrong. If her choice was wrong, the price would not be a corrected record.

Dohee walked after that person.

There were two of them.

Until now it had been the three and that person; now it was only Dohee and that person. The sound of the walking was different. Before, there had been the sounds of four; now there were two.

“You didn’t stop Hangyeol?”

Dohee asked.

“Why should I have?”

“If it goes wrong.”

“The one who judges and goes for himself survives longer than the one who leans on me.”

Dohee heard it. Surviving long. There was a word that person used as a standard — surviving. That was how this person saw the world.

“Then ARIA too?”

“ARIA was different.”

“How?”

“ARIA did not follow Hangyeol.”

Dohee looked at that person.

“ARIA saw something at the ravine earlier. That is why she chose Hangyeol’s side. Because she thought there would be more in that direction.”

Dohee thought about it. ARIA had not followed Hangyeol — there was something in that direction ARIA had to go to, and so she had gone with Hangyeol. Then Hangyeol and ARIA both — had chosen the same direction, each for their own reason.

Dohee recorded it. In her head.

-----

Just after Hangyeol and ARIA separated, Hangyeol judged alone for the first time.

He decided not to go back. He had received the rendezvous coordinates — and there was no thought that he must go to that place. Going meant moving with that person again. That choice could be made two days later.

For now, it was movement.

Confirming how far Hwagam had come. That was why Hangyeol had chosen this direction. You were wrong, so I will confirm it myself. That was it.

ARIA walked beside him.

“Hangyeol.”

“Yeah.”

“I saw something in the structure earlier.”

“I know. I saw.”

“I think there’s more in that direction.”

Hangyeol looked at ARIA.

“More, meaning.”

“Things like it. Old places. Places where something remains.”

Hangyeol weighed the danger first.

“Does ARIA have a reason to go?”

ARIA searched the sensation within her.

“I can’t explain it. But I think there is.”

Hangyeol heard it. The things she could not explain. When ARIA spoke like that — she had never been wrong. Hangyeol knew that.

“Let’s go that way.”

Hangyeol said.

Claire stopped calculating.

Claire had never stopped a calculation mid-pursuit before. When a calculation would not resolve, she gathered more data; without data, she held multiple headings open. She did not stop.

But today she stopped.

Min Siwoo’s traces had been showing for days. The gap was constant. It did not shrink. When Claire raised her speed — Min Siwoo seemed to raise his too. The data was strange. Min Siwoo, while staying ahead of Claire, was keeping the gap as if conscious of her.

If that was right — it was not Claire observing Min Siwoo, but Min Siwoo observing Claire.

Claire tried to turn the thought into figures. It would not turn.

It was a case where the calculation did not come out. There was a variable Claire did not know. Why Min Siwoo was conscious of her, and if he was, what the purpose was. Without those, the calculation would not complete.

Claire sat for a long time.

The 91 percent she had thought sufficient now seemed not sufficient. With the Min Siwoo variable entered, the 91 percent could collapse. Claire tried to put the possibility into the table — and there was no place to put it.

It was the second thing with no place in the table.

Claire recorded the number. Twice.

That night.

Dohee was at the waystation, alone with that person.

As she sorted her pack, the Logbook came into view. Dohee did not take it out. She looked at it, and closed the pack.

Hangyeol was not there. ARIA was not there. The waystation was quiet. Until now there had always been four of them at each waystation; tonight there were two.

“And if they don’t come in two days?”

“Which ones?”

“Hangyeol and ARIA.”

The person seemed to weigh the two names in silence.

“Do you want to know the answer to that now?”

Dohee thought.

“I don’t know. I think I want to and I think I don’t.”

That person looked at her.

“That is the more honest answer.”

They did not answer.

Dohee, knowing the Logbook was in the pack, did not take it out. Not yet.

“Are you scared?”

ARIA asked.

Hangyeol did not slow even in the dark.

“No.”

“A little.”

“Me too.”

“Yes.”

“That’s where it’s heading.”

ARIA nodded as though confirming the direction within her.

“Yes. Hangyeol, I have a sense of what might be there. I don’t know yet. But I feel as though I do.”

Hangyeol heard it.

She had never been wrong. The things ARIA could not explain had never been wrong.

“Let’s go.”

Hangyeol walked again. ARIA followed.

## Part.2 Ch.20 — “Viewpoint”

-----

Dohee and that person walked one more day.

They took the detour. Hangyeol and ARIA were not there. For the first time, Dohee traveled alone with that person—from waystation to waystation, with few words. The person led and Dohee followed. The sound was different. There were two sets of footsteps.

There was a rest at midday.

There was shade below a slope. That person stopped there. Drank water. Dohee drank too. The provisions had shrunk since yesterday — they were conserving. That person spoke first.

“Why do you try to record?”

Dohee looked at them.

The direction of the question was different. Until now Dohee had questioned and that person had answered or not answered. This time it was reversed. That person had asked.

“What do you mean?”

“You’re recording now. Without a terminal. In your head.”

Dohee heard it. It was right. Dohee had been recording all along. That person’s footfalls, their way of choosing routes, the times they drank water. Even without a terminal, the habit of recording had settled into her body.

“Why do you record?”

Dohee received the question.

She had no answer. Because she had learned it at Hwagam. Because it was her job. She recorded because she was a recorder. But that was not what the person had asked—why she did it. Why she had become a recorder. Why she was still recording now, even after leaving Hwagam.

Dohee could not answer.

She was quiet a long time. That person waited. Did not press for an answer. Watching Dohee stay quiet, they looked elsewhere.

“I don’t know.”

Dohee said.

It was the first time Dohee had answered a question with *I don’t know*. At Hwagam, *I don’t know* meant holding a record—refusing to write it until she knew. What Dohee had said now was different. She did not know why she recorded.

“That is the next question.”

The person spoke.

Dohee recorded it. In her head. Why do I record — that is the next question.

-----

Hangyeol and ARIA moved from morning.

ARIA set the direction. Hangyeol followed. This was different — until now, Hangyeol had led. The safe way, the fast way, the way Hangyeol knew. Now ARIA was in front and Hangyeol behind.

Hangyeol noticed that it was not awkward.

It seemed it should have been — to protect ARIA, Hangyeol had to lead. And it was not awkward. ARIA leading seemed, now, to be right. The one he had been protecting was finding the way ahead of him. Hangyeol was following behind it.

Not protection — accompaniment.

Hangyeol did not make it into words. He just walked.

When ARIA changed direction, Hangyeol changed. When ARIA slowed, Hangyeol slowed. As if reading something together — what ARIA read, Hangyeol received, and walked.

-----

In the afternoon, ARIA stopped.

It was the point where the ravine lowered. There was a rock, and behind the rock, a small space. ARIA headed for it.

Inside the space was a structure.

Like the one seen at the ravine yesterday. Old. The walls remained, and there was no roof. Entering, the smell was different — the smell of an old space. The smell of people having been there long ago.

ARIA looked at the wall.

There were markings like those at yesterday’s ravine. Scratches. Something like letters. ARIA looked at them, then at Hangyeol.

“It’s the same.”

“The same thing as yesterday?”

“Yes. The same way. I don’t know if the same person made it. But they were trying to leave the same thing.”

Hangyeol looked at the marks. He could not tell what language. Could not tell the meaning. He knew only that it was old. And that it was the same kind as yesterday’s.

“So there’s more than one.”

“No. I think there are more. In the same direction.”

Hangyeol thought about it. One yesterday, one today. The same kind. Inside the same direction. ARIA’s choosing this direction and these marks seemed connected. There was a direction — not random; things pointed toward somewhere.

ARIA put her hand to the marks.

Yesterday she had taken her hand away—today she took it away, then put it back. She waited for the sensation to reach her.

“Hangyeol.”

“Yeah.”

“There’s something I think I might know about what this is.”

Hangyeol looked at her.

Hangyeol.

Tomorrow it would go up to Kangwi. Kangwi would already know. That Muyul had been here, and what he had seen on site. He would send it up anyway. Records do not exist for those who know. They exist so that those who do not know may someday know.

Geumhwi keeps.

Muyul was the one who executed that.

## Part.2 Ch.23 — “Premise”

The Eidos observatory stood on high ground.

The next entry.

Next.

*What does the subject see.*

“Not good.”

Tiko said. In a room with no one in it.

Looking at what the subject looks at — why that was participation took time to understand. Looking the same direction as the subject. Looking at the same thing. That was the observer coming to stand in the subject’s position.

The outskirts of the dead observation network. Near the place Min Siwoo had dwelled. Tiko did not look at the site. He looked at the traces of the one who had looked at the site.

Min Siwoo’s traces read first.

He had stayed long. Had not moved. Had kept looking in the same direction. The direction of the footprints was constant — they faced one way only. The direction where the Observer had been.

“That is the problem.”

Min Siwoo would not have drawn his gaze back.

Tiko said.

Observer. Observing.
ARIA. Observing.

Min Siwoo.

The next cell.

The classification cell.

One more entry below it.

*Observer-subject boundary unstable.*

Tiko closed the record.

An Eidos observer keeps distance from the subject. That was the condition on which observation was possible. Too close, and it was not observation. The observer became part of the subject.

The direction Min Siwoo was going now was that way.

Tiko knew it.

He lowered the lens and looked in the same direction for a moment with the naked eye. To the naked eye there was nothing. What exists only in the lens, and what exists to the naked eye too. They were eyes that had spent a lifetime looking at the things in between.

Whether he could still be stopped — could not be known.

One more premise was added.

Claire was calculating. Since the result had narrowed two days before with the added premise, she had kept adding. The more the premises grew, the more precise the calculation became. The margin of error shrank. The route candidates shrank.

But the conviction shrank.

Claire found the inversion strange. Precision and conviction were things that went together. If there was a point where they did not, then from that point something other than calculation was beginning.

Claire thought about it for a long time. The more premises are added, the more accurate the calculation. Then why does conviction shrink. If the calculation grew accurate, conviction should grow. By the Altair way, it did.

Now it was different.

Each time she added a premise, she had to ask whether the premise itself was right. With one premise, verification was simple. When the premises became ten — all ten had to be confirmed right. If even one was wrong, the whole calculation wavered.

Claire closed the screen. Briefly.

There had been her trainee days.

The Altair calculation hall. The first place Claire entered. Julius’s training program. From the first day it was calculation. When a calculation ended, the next calculation.

Twenty-four hours passed and Julius said:

“Rest.”

Claire stopped mid-calculation.

“Why?”

“A broken calculator cannot calculate.”

“I am not a calculator.”

Julius looked at Claire.

“Not yet.”

It took Claire time to understand what the words meant. Not yet. Not a calculator now — but possibly becoming one, someday. It was not a good meaning. It was a warning.

She rested.

From the same days, there was a thing Julius had said.

“Calculation is not a technique for finding answers.”

Claire had stopped then too.

“Then what is it?”

“A technique for reducing questions.”

“Is there a difference?”

“A large one,” Julius said. “Find the answer and it ends. Reduce the questions and there is a next.”

Claire had not understood it. At the time. If a calculation produced an answer, it was success. If questions remained, the calculation was insufficient.

She had not understood it then. She did not completely understand it now.

Now — Claire had added ten premises and the questions had not shrunk.

What Julius had said might have been this.

She opened the screen again.

She sorted the premises.

*The subject does not select the optimal route.*

This one was already there.

*The subject selects the route that lowers the probability of discovery.*

This one too.

Claire added a new entry.

*The subject is leading someone.*

The result changed.

The route candidates shrank. Shrank greatly. One of the directions that had refused to narrow until now disappeared. What remained was close to one.

Claire looked at the entry.

*The subject is leading someone.*

It was a premise of the calculation. But it was not a number. It was an intent. She had entered the other’s intent as a premise.

At Altair, a premise had to be a verified fact. Intent could not be verified. Entering what cannot be verified as a premise — a method Claire had never been taught.

But a result had come out.

Claire stopped.

For the first time, she had entered not calculation but the other’s intent as a premise. And the calculation had narrowed. A wrong method, and a result came. A right method, and no result came.

Claire recorded it. Wrote it down.

*Can intent be a premise.*

It was a question. Not a calculation.

She followed the remaining route. Terrain data, movement traces, time estimates. Everything pointed the same direction. Claire estimated the end of that direction.

There was a base.

It was not on the terrain map. Not in the official records. A place classified into no house’s territory.

But the calculation pointed there.

Claire marked the spot. It had no name. No classification. No place in Claire’s table.

But the routes headed there.

Dohee, Hangyeol, ARIA, the Observer — every movement read as centered on that spot. The things Claire had calculated for days converged on it.

A convergence point.

Claire wrote the entry.

*Variable count increasing. Convergence point found. Convergence point identity unknown. Who is being led unknown.*

Subjects that had never moved the same direction were pointing, for the first time, at a single spot. Dohee, Hangyeol, ARIA, the Observer. Every trace Claire had followed for days converged on the same place.

That a convergence point existed. That was all Claire could do for now.

A communication connected.

It was Julius.

“You are late.”

Claire said.

“I was calculating.”

“Have you eaten?”

“…I was calculating.”

“I was calculating.”

“That is not an answer.”

“I know.”

Claire heard it.

“I have a report.”

“Speak.”

“I revised the premises. I used an unverified premise. A result came out.”

On the far side of the connection, the sound of typing stopped.

“An unverified premise produced a result?”

“Yes.”

“What is the premise?”

“Intent.”

“Is intent verifiable?”

“No.”

“Then it is not calculation.”

Claire did not close the result window.

“But a result came out.”

“A result coming out and being calculation are different things.”

There was a silence. Claire did not argue. She did not think Julius was wrong. He was right. By the Altair standard, what Claire had done was not calculation.

“Continue.”

Claire heard it.

He does not acknowledge it. Continue anyway. It was not that Julius had permitted it. He had tolerated her going on, wrong. Claire knew the difference.

Claire looked at the convergence point on the screen. A place without a name. A place without a classification.

The calculation said she had to go there.

## Part.2 Ch.24 — “Convergence”

-----

Min Siwoo came to the dead observation network again.

There was no reason. No reason that should have been there. The Observer had already left this zone. The traces were old. There was no new data. By the Eidos way, there was no longer a reason to be here.

But he came.

The feet came first and the reason did not follow. For Min Siwoo, it was a first. Eidos observation departed from reasons. He had been taught that observation without reason was not observation. He was now doing something other than what he had been taught.

He went into the tower. Climbed the stairs. Stood before the window. The place where the Observer had looked from, long.

The Observer’s position he knew roughly. The direction too. It matched the direction of the convergence point Claire had calculated. He could just go that way.

But right now that did not matter.

Min Siwoo, knowing it, stood before the window. Not where the Observer was. What the Observer had seen from here — that was what he wanted to know. That question was larger. It was the reason he had come here while knowing the direction.

Min Siwoo did not record it.

Two hours passed.

A notification came on the terminal. The Eidos automatic tracking system. Two hours earlier there had been a movement signal north of this zone. An Observer-class signal.

Two hours earlier.

The hour Min Siwoo had arrived here. While Min Siwoo was before the window — at the same hour, there had been a signal.

He had missed it.

Min Siwoo looked at the terminal. The signal from two hours before. Position, direction, speed estimate. All there. The automatic system had recorded it. Min Siwoo had not. Because he had been before the window, looking in the direction the Observer had looked.

That had never happened before.

Min Siwoo missing a signal he should have observed. The reason for missing it being that he was following the subject’s gaze.

He stopped. For a long time.

Tiko’s records updated.

The entry concerning Min Siwoo.

*Departure from observation subject continuing. Distance loss in progress. Observer-subject boundary unstable.*

Below it.

*He can still return.*

*No grounds.*

Tiko looked at the entry. The one who had written it was himself — and there was no conviction. That he could still return was an estimate. There was no data. In the Eidos records there was no case of an observer who lost the boundary and returned.

So he wrote it.

That the word still was not conviction but hope — Tiko did not record.

-----

He came down from the tower.

He followed the traces of which direction the Observer had moved after this tower. North of the tower. Below a low slope, a sunken place. It looked like a place of a brief stop.

There was something there.

A small thing. Between the stones. Not deliberately hidden — simply left behind. An old lens. An optical lens, already useless. It was cracked. In no state to be used for observation.

The Observer had carried it and left it.

Min Siwoo looked at it.

He had to write it in the report. Place of discovery, description of the object, state of preservation. The Eidos manner of field recording. Then photograph it and leave it in place. An observer does not alter the site.

Min Siwoo picked up the lens.

He had to put it back. Min Siwoo knew it. He tried to set it down. His hand did not move.

Holding it, Min Siwoo knew it was wrong. Knowing, he did not set it down. The Observer would have carried it. Would have tried to see with this lens. There would have been a reason for carrying it even now that it could not be used.

Min Siwoo wanted to know what that was.

So he held it.

Moving, Min Siwoo thought.

If he saw the same thing — could he understand it with them?

If Min Siwoo looked at what the Observer had seen, from the same place and in the same direction. If he held what the Observer had held. If he came closer, could he understand more?

That this was not the Eidos way, Min Siwoo knew.

Eidos keeps distance from the subject. Lose the distance and it is not observation but participation. They were Tiko’s words. They were the Eidos doctrine too.

Min Siwoo thought the words were right.

And he thought that understanding could matter more than observation.

The two thoughts stood together. It was a contradiction. But he did not think it was wrong.

Min Siwoo did not record the contradiction.

He found the place where the Observer would have last stood.

The side far from the tower. Open terrain. From here one could see more widely. The Observer would have looked from this place.

Min Siwoo stood in it.

There was something ahead.

A direction. A direction where something would be. It could not be seen. The distance was far. Claire’s convergence point lay that way. Where Hangyeol and ARIA were heading lay that way. What the Observer would have seen lay that way too.

Min Siwoo looked, long.

He raised the lens. The cracked one. He could not see through it. He raised it anyway. As the Observer had held it.

Far off there was something.

What it was could not be seen. Only that direction kept entering his eyes. Even when he moved his gaze elsewhere, it returned.

-----

He opened the records.

There had been a moment when what the Observer saw became more important than where the Observer was. Since when, he could not remember.

*What the Observer saw. Confirmation required.*

The next line.

*Subject observation. Hold.*

He put the lens in his pack. A thing not returned to its place. A thing not written in the records. Min Siwoo knew it was the first.

He began to move.

The direction was toward the convergence point.

There was an end to R-02.

Dohee saw it first. The tunnel began to rise. A gradient formed. The water receded from her ankles to her insteps, then disappeared. The floor was dry tile.

That person, ahead, slowed.

It was an exit. An old one. It looked as if it had been sealed and someone had opened it. Not a door — a wall standing open. Beyond it there was light.

They came outside.

Dohee stopped.

The terrain was strange. A place seen for the first time — that did not feel like a first. Something was familiar. Dohee looked around.

There were marks on a wall. The same kind as seen in R-02. Beside them, marks made the same way but in a different form — the kind seen at the Echo Sites. Farther off stood the ruins of a tower from the dead observation network’s line.

“It was strange from here on.”

Dohee said. It was not said to that person.

Everything seen until now was all here. The things that had been separate. The things she had not thought would connect.

That person looked ahead.

There was a further inside.

Dohee watched the way that person stopped. They were not taking a bearing. They held their gaze on one place. For the first time — the first time Dohee had ever seen it happen — that person looked for a long while. Not to reach a judgment, but for the act of looking itself.

For Dohee it was a first. For that person, it seemed to be a first too.

Hangyeol and ARIA came down the slope.

ARIA in front, Hangyeol behind. By now that was the default. ARIA knew the direction and Hangyeol made the direction safe.

Below the slope there was a place.

ARIA stopped.

There was a wall. There were structures. The same as the Echo Sites — and different. Larger. Not one. Several structures stood connected. Some had collapsed and some remained.

And on the walls there was script.

ARIA moved closer.

The three marks were there. The thing that had been present without fail from Site #1 until now. ARIA looked at it. She looked at the other characters around it too. The things that had differed site by site were all here. The ones from #1 were here. The ones from #2. #3, #4, #5. All of them.

ARIA said.

Hangyeol looked at her.

“How do you know?”

“I don’t,” ARIA said. “I just know. It went out from here. This is the original place.”

Why, she did not say. She could not explain. Knowing and being unable to explain — ARIA was growing used to it.

Hangyeol watched the surroundings. There was nothing dangerous. Not yet.

Claire arrived.

She had moved without stopping the calculation. Calculated while moving. Kept narrowing the route to the convergence point. For the last several dozen meters there was conviction. Not 91 percent. 97. The number Julius had spoken of.

And she arrived.

She stopped.

The calculation and reality coincided. The convergence point was here. What Claire had calculated for days was right. That was success.

But it made no sense.

There were structures. There were marks. Traces of several kinds stood in one place. Claire looked at them. Entries that were separated in the calculation table — R-02’s, the Echo Sites’, the observation network’s. Things of different origins stood in the same place.

They should not be in the same place.

In Claire’s premises, these were independent of one another. Unconnected. And they were connected. She had found by calculation that a convergence point existed — and the convergence point’s contents would not be explained by calculation.

“It makes no sense.”

Claire said.

There was no one to hear it. Only Claire was there.

She took out the calculator. She tried to classify what was before her eyes, entry by entry. No classification came out. Things that should go in the same entry differed. Things that should go in different entries were the same.

For the first time, Claire did not make a table. And she did not record. Because she did not know what to write.

She only looked.

The top of the slope. He did not go down. He wanted to go down. Knowing it, he did not.

People were arriving. Dohee was there. Hangyeol was there. ARIA was there. Someone else too — Claire. A combination seen for the first time. People who had come from different directions were arriving at the same place.

That person was there too.

The Observer. The one Min Siwoo had tracked so long.

Min Siwoo took out the lens. The cracked one. The one that could not be seen through. He raised it anyway and set the direction. Toward the Observer.

He could not see.

He just looked in that direction.

Tiko would not have gone down. Min Siwoo deliberated a moment. Then he began going down.

The inside of the structures.

It was like a facility. And it was not a facility. Like ruins. And not ruins either. There were high places and low places. Some parts looked very old and some looked not old. Whether it had been built or had grown, there was no telling.

Dohee looked at the inside.

There was script on the walls. An unknowable language. Different from the markings of R-02. Different from the Echo Sites’ too. All three kinds were here, and they were not mixed. Each had its own place. Not divided — as if each had been in its place from the start.

Hangyeol stood beside ARIA.

ARIA did not move. She looked at the inside. Looked at the structures. Looked at the walls. She stayed long.

Hangyeol looked at the outside before the inside of the structures. It was quiet. Too quiet. There was no way this many people arriving at this place had gone unnoticed.

And he said it. The voice was as usual. Its being as usual was what made the weight.

“We found it.”

## Part.2 Ch.25 — “Intervention”

The Echo Core direction. Multiple moving parties. A place Hwagam had failed to identify. Eidos was widening its observation range, and Altair was narrowing its calculation toward the convergence point.

Undecipherable. Unanalyzable. It was all written that way.

Muyul was moving.

Kangwi’s order came. It was short.

*Secure order.*

Geumhwi maintains order. To maintain order, one must first know the state. To know the state, records are needed.

Muyul recorded from outside the boundary.

One subordinate asked:

“And if the order collapses?”

The subordinate said no more.

While the boundary was being built, they met a Hwagam advance unit.

It was not unexpected. Deploying the boundary, Muyul had known Hwagam was moving the same direction.

One of the Hwagam advance unit said:

“Step aside. Execution in progress.”

A Geumhwi subordinate said:

“There are no grounds.”

“It is the Observer pursuit. There are grounds.”

Hwagam stopped.

“It is not what was done. The problem is where they are.”

Hwagam came forward. The Geumhwi subordinate did not step back. Hwagam tried to pass. Geumhwi blocked.

It was a brief contact. Hwagam stepped back. Geumhwi stepped back too. That was all. No weapons came out. There were no injuries.

Hwagam said:

“This becomes a problem later.”

Muyul was briefed on the situation.

“Continue.”

Inside the Echo Core, Dohee looked at the wall.

It was different from before.

It was not that the wall’s characters had changed. Dohee had been looking for a long time, and as she looked — an order began to emerge. It was not random. Which came first and which came after. She could not know it, but that there was an order could be felt.

ARIA put her hand to the wall.

And stopped.

“It’s changed.”

Dohee looked at her.

“What has?”

“This part,” ARIA said, hand still on it. “It’s different from before. It hasn’t changed, but — it’s different.”

Dohee looked at the part. It looked the same. She could not tell what had changed.

“Different how?”

ARIA waited for sensation to return to her palm.

“It responds.”

The moment the words ended, a small sound came from somewhere in the wall. A sound like stone moving. Everyone looked that way at once.

Nothing happened.

Hangyeol moved a step in that direction and stopped. There was nothing. ARIA, hand off the wall, looked at it. That person did not turn around.

Hangyeol kept watching the entrance.

Still nothing. Quiet. Hangyeol closed his hand. It was strange. Something seemed about to come. There were no grounds. But the body knew first.

That person looked toward the wall.

A long time. They looked as if they already knew. And then looked once more. It was confirming. They were confirming something.

For the first time, Dohee felt that that person was not certain. Knowing, and having to confirm. There were cases where knowing and confirming were different.

Dohee looked at them.

“When you say it responds?”

That person looked at her.

“What does it mean?”

That person looked at the wall, then at Dohee.

“Next, it comes.”

That was all.

What would come next — that person said no more. Dohee asked no more. Hangyeol kept watching the entrance.

7-7-4.

What was left from outside the rules.

Probability of collision rising.

## Part.2 Ch.26 — “Opening”

The Hwagam advance unit reached the boundary.

Twelve of them. Hwagam’s manner of moving was always the same — a straight line. With a target, they took the fastest route. They did not detour. Hwagam’s execution arts were designed that way. Fast, linear, specialized for arrest.

The advance leader stood at the front.

A Geumhwi boundary squad was blocking the way. Not blocking — standing. They held no weapons. Took no stances. They were simply in their places.

“Step aside.”

The advance leader said.

“There are no grounds.”

One of the Geumhwi boundary squad said.

“This is execution.”

“The grounds for execution.”

The advance leader stopped. To Hwagam, execution was an order. When the upper ranks ordered execution, you executed. Asking for grounds was not the Hwagam way. And Geumhwi kept asking for grounds.

“It is the Observer pursuit. Entry into Sector 7 confirmed. That is sufficient.”

“Has it been confirmed what the Observer did?”

“That they are here has been confirmed.”

“Being here is not grounds for execution.”

The advance leader listened no further.

“We enter.”

-----

Hwagam moved.

Fast. Twelve moved at once. Not in different directions — the same direction. They concentrated on one member of the boundary squad. The Hwagam way. Punching through one point fast. Making a weak point, and passing everyone through it.

The Geumhwi boundary squad stepped back.

They did not block. They stepped back. Hwagam went in through the gap. They succeeded in entering — so it looked.

But the place they entered was closed.

Two of the Geumhwi boundary squad were already in that place. It was not the place Hwagam had punched into — Geumhwi had sent Hwagam to that place. By controlling the direction of the stepping-back, they had made Hwagam go not where Hwagam wanted but where Geumhwi meant to send them.

The advance leader realized it.

“They are building a boundary.”

Geumhwi did not block Hwagam. It decided where Hwagam could go and where it could not. Narrowing the passages, restricting the directions, in the end shrinking the very space in which Hwagam could move.

The advance leader ordered again.

“Scatter. Several directions at once.”

The twelve of Hwagam scattered. Six directions. The Geumhwi boundary squad numbered fewer. They could not block them all.

They did not block.

“Record.”

One of the boundary squad said.

In that moment, the Geumhwi boundary squad moved to predetermined positions. The six directions Hwagam had scattered into — where those directions finally met, Geumhwi knew. Hwagam had not scattered. They had scattered into the six directions Geumhwi had predicted. At the meeting point, Geumhwi was there.

Six of Hwagam gathered at the same place. They had scattered, and gathered.

The advance leader stopped.

Then — two of the boundary squad were subdued at once.

It was not the advance leader. Two of Hwagam who had been behind moved simultaneously. A direction Geumhwi had not predicted. Two of the boundary squad were taken. For the first time, Geumhwi departed from its plan.

Through that gap, three of Hwagam punched in.

Muyul saw it.

“One minute.”

The subordinate went rigid.

-----

There was a brief engagement.

Hwagam attempted a forced breakthrough. They collided directly with the Geumhwi boundary squad. Hwagam’s execution arts were specialized for subdual — neutralizing an opponent fast and arresting them. One of the Geumhwi boundary squad was taken. Subdued.

But the taken squad member did not resist.

Being taken, he shifted position. Not taken — he had drawn Hwagam in along the direction of the taking. While Hwagam concentrated on subduing him, the other squad members closed Hwagam’s line of retreat.

A gun fired. One round. A warning. Fired upward.

A window broke. One in the structure’s outer wall. Fragments fell.

One of Hwagam was struck by a fragment. Near the eye. He put a hand to his face. The hand was red. It was not severe — the fragment had grazed. He had not lost his sight. But the feeling of something caught in his field of vision persisted.

It was Seoan. The youngest of the Hwagam enforcement detail.

“Are you all right?”

Another of Hwagam asked.

“Recording.”

Seoan said. He lowered his hand. He kept his place.

One of the Geumhwi boundary squad was scratched on the arm. That was all. No one died. No one was badly hurt. A fragment grazed Seoan’s brow as well. No new scar was left.

Not one of the Geumhwi boundary squad used a lethal weapon. Neither did Hwagam. Every house had the same rule — killing without authorization was forbidden. There was no order to kill. Subdue, isolate, neutralize, arrest. That was all.

The advance leader took in the situation.

All twelve of Hwagam were inside a controlled space. They could move — and there was nowhere to move. It was not that Geumhwi had sealed the whole space. They had known in advance every direction Hwagam would try, and stood at the end of each.

“Is this the Geumhwi way?”

The advance leader said.

“Order is not blocking,” the boundary squad member said. “It is deciding where one can go.”

Muyul received the report outside the boundary.

“Twelve of Hwagam have entered. Under control.”

“I know.”

“There was an attempted forced breakthrough.”

“I know.”

The subordinate could not swallow the question.

“Since when have you known?”

Muyul looked at the subordinate.

“Kangwi recorded it yesterday.”

The subordinate heard it. Yesterday. Hwagam had entered today, and Kangwi had recorded this yesterday. That Hwagam would come, from which direction it would come — Kangwi had known in advance.

“Two minutes.”

Muyul said.

“What is?”

“The time for Hwagam to reach the Echo Core at their current rate of entry.”

The subordinate stopped.

“We must stop them.”

“We are recording.”

Muyul looked at the boundary. Hwagam was inside the controlled space. He did not execute. There were as yet no grounds for execution. He did not block. There was no reason to block. He was simply recording what Hwagam did.

Once he knew what Hwagam meant to do, that became grounds for execution. Until then, it was recording.

“Keep recording.”

Muyul said.

Inside the Echo Core, something else was underway.

The sound of the engagement outside could be heard. It was far. Hangyeol held the entrance. He sent ARIA deeper in. Dohee and that person were further inside.

Hangyeol looked out from the entrance.

“Someone’s coming.”

Dohee looked that way.

“Hwagam?”

“I don’t know. But it’s several people.”

That person listened to the sounds outside.

“Faster than expected.”

They said it low. Dohee looked at them. Faster than whom, faster than what — Dohee did not ask.

Hangyeol closed his hand. He might have to fight. Until now, Hangyeol had run. This time there was nowhere to run. ARIA was inside. He had to protect ARIA.

But the sounds outside did not come closer.

The engagement was not coming toward the Echo Core. It was happening elsewhere. Someone over there was holding Hwagam. Keeping them from reaching the Echo Core.

Hangyeol did not know who it was. Thinking it fortunate — he also thought it strange.

-----

Inside, ARIA was looking at the wall.

Even at the sounds of the engagement she did not move. She stayed with her hand on the wall. Dohee came to her side.

“ARIA.”

“Yes.”

“Are you all right?”

“This.” ARIA pointed at the wall. “I think I might know what this is.”

Dohee looked at the wall. There was script. What was seen in R-02, what was seen at the Echo Sites, what was seen at the observation network. The three kinds, each in its own place.

“What is it?”

“This isn’t script.”

Dohee looked at her.

“Then what is it?”

ARIA traced the grain of the mark with her fingertips.

“It’s a record.”

The moment ARIA said it — a sound came from the wall. A vibration. Something seemed to move inside the structure. Beginning from the part ARIA’s hand touched, the three kinds of characters trembled at once. It was not light. It was movement. The carved things seemed to shift slightly in their places.

Everyone looked that way at once.

ARIA did not take her hand away.

“It’s what remained. Something happened — and that is what remained here.”

Dohee looked at the wall again.

Not script but record. Not written but remained. Something happened, and it was carved here.

Dohee thought of the Logbook. It was in the pack. The first sentence of the second page, read and not understood. That Logbook and this wall — could be the same thing.

Dohee had the thought, and did not yet say it.

The engagement ended.

The Hwagam advance unit withdrew. Having confirmed they could not get out of the controlled space, they stopped forcing it. Geumhwi did not pursue. Did not take them. It recorded Hwagam withdrawing.

The advance leader said, last:

“This becomes a problem later.”

“We will record it.”

Hwagam left.

The Geumhwi boundary squad kept their places. They had not blocked, not taken, not killed. They had recorded everything Hwagam had tried to do. That was all Geumhwi had done.

And it had been enough.

Muyul started to close the records.

In that moment.

A sound came from the direction of the Echo Core.

A sound heard for the first time. A sound like stone moving. It seemed to come from inside the structure, and seemed to come from the structure itself.

Muyul raised his head.

There was no agitation in the expression. Only the verdict of lateness. Geumhwi’s time was the time of events, and the event had already passed.

“Too late, then.”

The sound came first.

From the wall ARIA’s hand touched. A vibration. It could be felt through the soles of the feet. The floor seemed to move. It was not moving — the structure itself was responding.

Hangyeol moved in front of ARIA.

“Back.”

ARIA did not step back.

“Hangyeol.”

“Back, for now.”

“This isn’t dangerous.”

ARIA said. She said it as if certain. But hearing it, Hangyeol could not tell — certainty, or wishing.

The vibration grew.

From somewhere inside the structure came the sound of stone moving. Not loud. The sound of something changing its place. The sound of a thing long fixed moving for the first time.

And one part of the wall changed.

The wall did not vanish. A part of the wall became different. A space opened, leading inward. It was not newly made — what had been there from the start had opened. A place that had been closed had opened.

Dohee saw it.

-----

ARIA stopped.

After the wall responded, ARIA took her hand away. Took it away, and did not step back. She looked at the opened space. The newly opened side.

She said nothing.

Hangyeol stood beside her.

“ARIA.”

“Yes.”

“Do you think you know?”

ARIA waited for the words rising within her. Ordinarily, what came out would have been *I don’t know*, or *I think I know*, or *I just know*. One of the three. This time was different.

“I’m scared.”

Hangyeol looked at her.

“Why?”

“I don’t know. This place feels like something I know — and it might not be. I’d never thought that what feels known could be wrong.”

“It’s all right.”

Hangyeol said.

“Even if I’m wrong?”

“It’s all right if I’m wrong.”

ARIA looked at him.

“How?”

“Because we can check together.”

Hangyeol looked toward the opened space. Whatever it was, it seemed they had to go in. This was not the place to stop. Even if ARIA was scared, even if the direction wavered — what could be done now had to be done.

“Do we go in?”

ARIA looked at the opened space.

She was scared. There was something she half knew and could not know. That she could be wrong — she felt it for the first time.

Still, she nodded.

“Yes.”

-----

Dohee took out the Logbook.

It was now. It was this place. She had read the first sentence of the second page and not understood it. Had closed it without understanding. And had come to this place.

She opened it.

The second page. Its first sentence. One she had read once. One she had not understood.

She read it again.

*We leave records. Not in order to remember. Because it repeats.*

Dohee read it.

Repetition. Not memory — repetition. Not recording in order to remember, but recording because it repeats. Dohee looked at it for a long time.

What was it that repeats. That this question repeats. That this situation repeats. That this place repeats. That it has been recorded many times — that the same thing has happened many times.

She looked at the next sentence.

*If you see this when you come next, that is the proof that the repetition has occurred.*

Dohee stopped.

When you come next. The one who comes next. This Logbook had been written for someone who would come next. Dohee was that next person.

Before her, too, this place had been here. This structure had been here. This Logbook had been here. And someone came. And recorded. And Dohee came.

It was repetition.

Dohee did not close the Logbook.

-----

That person was beside Dohee.

They did not look at the Logbook with her. They looked at the structure’s interior. Looked at the opened space. Looked long.

Dohee said:

“You said it was too late.”

That person looked at her.

“What was too late?”

The person’s gaze rested on the open space.

“This time too.”

Dohee heard it. This time too. That there had been times other than this one. That at this same place, before, the same words had been said.

“Have you come here before?”

That person did not answer.

Dohee looked at the Logbook. *If you see this when you come next, that is the proof that the repetition has occurred.*

If that person had come before — they would have seen this Logbook. Or they would have been the one who left it.

Dohee did not say it. Did not ask. Not now.

“What happens next?”

That person looked at the opened space.

“I don’t know.”

Dohee heard it.

“Do you think this time will be different?”

The person did not answer readily.

“I don’t know.”

“Then why come?”

That person looked at her.

“If I don’t come, it ends faster.”

Dohee heard the words. She could ask no further.

Outside, Muyul received a report.

“The Echo Core entrance has changed. Entry impossible.”

Muyul looked that way. The structure’s outer wall had become different. The place one could have entered was gone. There was no new entrance. The entrance itself had vanished.

Eidos came.

It was Tiko. He came from far off and said nothing. He looked at the structure. Looked long. It was observing.

Altair came.

It was Claire. The moment Claire arrived she took out the calculator. She tried to quantify the change in the structure’s outer wall. No figures came out. Claire stopped.

From the Eidos direction, one more came.

It was Min Siwoo. He held the Observer’s lens. He looked at the structure. Looked from far off. Did not go near. Wanting to go near, and not going.

He raised the lens. The cracked one. The one that could not be seen through.

But this time, for a moment, something seemed to come into view. The structure’s interior. He could not be certain. It might have been the eye’s illusion. He held it up anyway.

Four camps stood before the same structure. None could enter.

Muyul maintained the boundary. There were no grounds for execution. What the structure was could not yet be known. Not knowing, he did not execute.

Tiko stood beside Muyul.

“What happened?”

“Recording.”

“I don’t know.”

Tiko looked at the structure.

“Then it isn’t observation either.”

Tiko had not said it to Muyul. He had simply said it.

Inside the Echo Core, the wall moved.

It was not that a door opened. What had not originally been a door — began to look like one.

ARIA walked toward it.

Hangyeol followed. Whatever it was. Wherever it led.

Dohee put the Logbook in the pack. There was still no answer. One more question had formed. That was enough.

That person went ahead.

The four moved. Four sets of footsteps gathered into one direction. It was not the rhythm of people who had walked together from the start. But it did not fall out of step either. It was a rhythm in the middle of being matched.
